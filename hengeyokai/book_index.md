# Hengeyokai: Shapeshifters of the East — Index

## Important: an earlier-edition crossover book — read this first
This book (White Wolf, 1998) was written to pair with the **original,
pre-20th-Anniversary edition of *Werewolf: The Apocalypse*** (2nd
edition-era rules — the credits page trademark list includes *Werewolf
Players Guide*, *Freak Legion*, *Book of the Wyrm*, and the *Stargazers
Tribebook*, all classic-line titles) and with the **original 1998
*Kindred of the East*** line, not any later edition of either game.
Concretely:

- **Likely some inconsistency with *Werewolf: The Apocalypse 20th
  Anniversary Edition* (W20).** Terminology, Gift/Rite/Fetish systems,
  and Changing Breed rosters were revised for W20; this book predates
  that revision by roughly two decades and was never updated for it.
  Where this book's hengeyokai material overlaps with W20 material —
  for example, both books cover the Kitsune, and W20's own Kitsune
  writeup (Chapter Nine: Allies) is a compressed, independently-written
  summary rather than a reprint of this book's much fuller treatment —
  expect terminology and mechanical details to drift rather than match
  exactly.
- **Very likely significant inconsistency with *Kindred of the East:
  The Relentless Age*.** This book treats the Kuei-jin as a stable,
  antagonist/rival presence using the original 1998 *Kindred of the
  East* line's cosmology and vocabulary (Kuei-jin, Dharma, Yin/Yang,
  the Yomi World, Yama Kings). *The Relentless Age* is a separate,
  fan-made V20-era reimagining that renames the species entirely
  ("Hungry Dead"), replaces the soul system, and restructures nearly
  everything else — see that book's own bundle for the full list of
  divergences. Any comparison between this book's Kuei-jin material and
  *The Relentless Age* should be treated as comparing two unrelated
  continuities that happen to share a premise, not as evidence of
  either book being "wrong."
- **This is explicitly a crossover book**, similar in spirit to *Wind
  from the East* (which crosses *Kindred of the East* material into
  *Vampire: The Dark Ages*) — here the crossing runs the other
  direction, folding *Kindred of the East* cosmology and the Kuei-jin
  into a *Werewolf: The Apocalypse* sourcebook. It is a *Werewolf* book
  first and foremost (its own credits list only *Werewolf the
  Apocalypse* among the trademarked game lines, not *Kindred of the
  East* by name), written for Storytellers who want to run Asian
  Changing Breeds and cross paths with the Kuei-jin, not a *Kindred of
  the East*-line sourcebook itself.
- **Practical guidance:** if a query pulls a chunk from this book
  alongside chunks from a W20-era or Relentless-Age-era book, don't
  silently merge them into one answer — name which edition/continuity
  each fact comes from.

## How to use this file
This is a thematic index. For exact quotes, precise game statistics
(Gift/Rite levels, Willpower/Rage/Gnosis costs, dice pools), or
verifying specific rules text, query `book_chunks.db` (see `README.md`
in this bundle for query examples) rather than relying on the summaries
below. Page numbers below are the PDF's actual page numbers. This PDF
is an OCR scan (FineReader) rather than a native digital text layer;
the book's own decorative chapter-title fonts did not OCR at all
(chapter boundaries below were verified by visually inspecting each
chapter's title page, not by searching for chapter-name text), and a
handful of running headers, footers, and stylized pull-quotes came
through garbled. Body text is generally clean.

## Overview
*Hengeyokai: Shapeshifters of the East* is a *Werewolf: The Apocalypse*
sourcebook detailing Asian Changing Breeds — collectively the
**hengeyokai** ("Changing Phantoms") — who never experienced the War of
Rage the way Western Garou did, and instead cooperate through a shared
political/spiritual structure called the **Beast Courts of the Emerald
Mother** (their name for Gaia). The book covers seven non-Kitsune
hengeyokai races in depth (Chapter Three), the hengeyokai's own version
of the Umbra and cosmology (Chapters One–Two), magic items and
antagonists including the Kuei-jin and Yama King-led Wyrm forces
(Chapter Four), and then dedicates more than a third of the book
(Chapters Five through Eight, plus the Appendix) to a full, standalone
treatment of the **Kitsune** (werefoxes) — character creation, society,
and templates — because, per the book's own aside (p. 109), this
material began life as a planned separate Kitsune Changing Breed Book
before being folded into this one.

## Legends of the Garou: An Inauspicious Burial (pp. 12–15)
**Summary:** A framing fiction piece, not rules text. Michael
Walks-on-Ashes, a Stargazer Theurge from a California sept (the "Second
Seven" pack, bound to the Waking Seals Caern), travels to Yunnan, China
to bury the remains of his teacher's old friend, a Li family sage and
astrologer, on ancestral ground. He's challenged by local hengeyokai
watchmen and must prove himself worthy of the errand before being
allowed to complete the burial rite — establishing early that Western
Garou are outsiders in the East who must earn trust and follow local
protocol, not assume authority.
**Key themes:** the Sunset People (Western Garou) as guests, not
authorities, in hengeyokai territory; ancestor reverence; earning trust
through ritual correctness.
**Key terms/entities:** Michael Walks-on-Ashes, Stargazers, Waking
Seals Caern, Li Feng.

## Introduction: The Changing Phantoms (pp. 16–21)
**Summary:** Explains why Asian shapeshifters diverge from Western
Garou lore: the War of Rage never reached Asia the way it did the
West — instead, the hengeyokai lost heavily to the Bane-lords called
the **Yama Kings**, and as a result cooperate across breeds much more
readily than Western Changing Breeds do. Includes explicit Storyteller
guidance ("we strongly recommend you wait until the moment is right"
p. 9) cautioning against casually dropping hengeyokai into a standard
Werewolf chronicle, since most Garou never encounter Eastern
shapeshifters (or even a Western Bastet) in their lifetimes — the East
is meant to feel genuinely distant and exotic even in-setting. Closes
with an extensive glossary translating hengeyokai terminology to
Western-Garou equivalents: the five hengeyokai **auspices** — Fist
(Ahroun), Mirror (Theurge), Pillar (Galliard), Lantern (Philodox), Leaf
(Ragabash) — plus terms like Beast Courts of the Emerald Mother (Garou
Nation equivalent), Court (sept), Sentai (pack/war party), Dragon Nest
(caern), the Tapestry (Umbra), the Wall (Gauntlet), Wheel of Ages
(the great cosmic cycle, their version of the Ages), Fifth/Sixth Age
(current age / Apocalypse), Sunset People (Western shapeshifters),
Namebreakers (mages), Kin-jin (Western vampires), Kuei-jin (Eastern
vampires), Gaki (Japanese term for Kuei-jin), Bakemono (fomori), and
Okuma ("the Gurahl of the East," said to have been wiped out in an
earlier "War of Shame").
**Key themes:** the East as genuinely foreign to Western Garou
assumptions, not a reskin; breed cooperation over Western-style
inter-tribal rivalry.
**Key terms/entities:** hengeyokai, Yama Kings, the five hengeyokai
auspices (Fist/Mirror/Pillar/Lantern/Leaf), Beast Courts of the Emerald
Mother, War of Shame, Okuma.

## Chapter One: Lands of the Emerald Mother (pp. 22–40)
**Summary:** Covers hengeyokai history and geography. Explains the
**Wheel of Ages** cosmology (their version of a repeating cosmic
cycle rather than a single linear history), the Age of Testing (the
Fourth Age, an era of war and strife), and how the Beast Courts formed
in response to shared threats. Details the mandates that bind
hengeyokai to the Courts — obedience to the Mother's Court, honoring
ancestors and elders, honoring the pact with the spirit world, and the
Final Mandate (never abandoning the sacred places) — each given with a
"Reality" callout noting how well (or poorly) hengeyokai actually live
up to it in practice. Surveys the regions of the Middle Kingdom and
beyond: the Mirror Lands section explains how the Umbra's reflection of
the physical world differs across the East versus the West (spirits of
the Orient retain stronger ties to the physical world; Western-style
"disenchantment" spreads more slowly), and later pages survey specific
territories (an extended "This is the land of dragons" regional
passage, likely introducing Zhong Lung territory) and note that India
is only partially considered Beast Courts territory.
**Key themes:** obligation and its gap from practice (the
mandate/Reality structure); the East as spiritually "thicker" or less
disenchanted than the West; shared threat (the Yama Kings, the Kuei-jin)
as the glue holding a very diverse set of breeds together.
**Key terms/entities:** Wheel of Ages, Age of Testing, Mirror Lands,
Beast Courts mandates, Final Mandate.

## Chapter Two: The Tapestry (pp. 41–53)
**Summary:** The hengeyokai's equivalent of the Werewolf corebook's
Spirit World chapter. Describes **the Tapestry** (the Middle Umbra as
experienced in the East) as a dream-land richer in old spirits and
dragon-lore than its Western counterpart, still fading as the Fifth Age
wanes but not yet hollowed out the way parts of the Western Umbra are.
Covers **the Wall** (the Eastern Gauntlet) and its gates, **Dragon
Lines** (the hengeyokai's caern-connecting ley lines, functionally
paralleling Moon Bridges/Byways), and **Dragon Nests** (caerns) —
including a "Walking the Mountain's Shoulders" sidebar on sentai
Umbral travel privileges. Also details **the Mirror Lands** (the
Eastern Penumbra) at length: their tighter connection to the physical
world, the corrupting effect of Weaver influence via modern technology
weakening the old ways, the greater danger and awareness of Eastern
Mirror-Land spirits compared to Western Penumbral spirits, and how
Blights, Demon Realms (Hellholes), Epiphs, and Glens all appear in
Eastern flavor.
**Key themes:** parallel-but-different spirit geography; the East's
spirit world as older, more dangerous, and less eroded by modernity
than the West's, at least for now.
**Key terms/entities:** the Tapestry, the Wall, Dragon Line, Dragon
Nest, the Mirror Lands.

## Chapter Three: Lords of the Beast Courts (pp. 54–92)
**Summary:** The chapter's largest section — profiles of seven
non-Kitsune hengeyokai races (Kitsune get their own Chapters Five
through Eight). Opens (pp. 42–53) with the concept of **sentai**
(hengeyokai war parties: mountain sentai, bound by shared totem and
enduring like a Garou pack, versus looser wave sentai), the five-fold
"Wind"/element/task alignment system sentai members take on (echoing
but not identical to Garou auspices), and general Beast Courts social
mechanics. Then, roughly two-to-six pages each, in this order:
- **Hakken** (pp. 54–58 area; werewolves/weredogs, tied to totems like
  Susanowo "Lord of Storms" — noted as possibly Wyrm-corrupted — and
  Tsuki-yomi, "another name for Luna") — opens with the myth "Laughing
  Daughter of Night" about a samurai-clan Hakken cub named Sayo.
- **Khan** (weretigers) — opens with the myth "The King Who Forgot,"
  a tragic story of a tiger-slaying king whose son is transformed and
  kills him; the Khan have a Chatro ("Feline" Crinos-equivalent) form.
- **Kumo** (werespiders, explicitly related to the Werewolf corebook's
  Ananasi) — the text states plainly that, like Ananasi, all Kumo
  ultimately serve the Wyrm, though the race has internal factions with
  different totems (Rati "the Insatiable Lady" for the Yang Kumo,
  Marawa "the Destroyer" for the Yin Kumo, Nareau "the Spider Lord" for
  more balanced Kumo) and forms named Lilian and Aghora.
- **Nagah** (wereserpents) — opens with a creation-myth-style origin
  chant; the chapter gives multiple named serpent forms (Silkaram, Azhi
  Dahaka, Kali Dahaka, Vasuki) and Gifts including a healing gift,
  Breath of Sweet Amrita.
- **Nezumi** (wererats/mice) — opens with marketplace/urban imagery
  fitting their theme; Crinos-equivalent form given as "Rodens."
- **Same-Bito** (weresharks) — opens with the myth of Mizuchi, a young
  shark-shapeshifter taught wisdom by a Zhong Lung elder, Meiying, and
  brought into contact with the "lords of the sharks."
- **Tengu** (wereravens/crow-people) — opens with a grandfather's tale
  of a Tengu from the frozen mountains; Crinos-equivalent form given as
  "Corvid."
- **Zhong Lung** (weredragons, explicitly compared to the Werewolf
  corebook's Mokolé as "relatives") — the last and most detailed
  profile, with its own in-book "Zhong Lung Lexicon" glossary (terms
  like Archid/Chi'lung/Ching-tu/Chuming/Draco/K'ulueng breed and rank
  terms) before a full Description section; male Zhong Lung are called
  K'ulueng, female Ch'i'lung.
Each race's profile follows a broadly similar pattern: an origin myth,
a Description section, Stereotypes of the other hengeyokai races (see
example on p. 58 for the Khan's view of all the others), Gifts, and
game traits.
**Key themes:** each race mapped loosely (not identically) onto a
Western Changing Breed analogue (Kumo≈Ananasi, Zhong Lung≈Mokolé,
Same-Bito≈Rokea-adjacent, Okuma≈Gurahl per the Introduction glossary)
while keeping distinct Eastern myth-cycles and internal politics;
mutual stereotyping as the social glue (and friction point) of the
Beast Courts.
**Key terms/entities:** Hakken, Khan, Kumo, Nagah, Nezumi, Same-Bito,
Tengu, Zhong Lung, sentai, Gai'ran (court officials).

## Chapter Four: Weapons and Antagonists (pp. 93–107)
**Summary:** Opens with character-option material: Merits and Flaws
themed to hengeyokai life (Perfect Protocol, Auspicious Birth, Rival
Clan, Inauspicious Birth among them) and a discussion of why hengeyokai
don't map cleanly onto Western martial-arts genre tropes despite
surface similarities. Covers totems suitable for mountain sentai
(Chimera, Rat, Grandfather Thunder, Cockroach, Hare) and their
relationships to the various races (e.g., Hare's tense relationship
with the Kitsune). The back half of the chapter is a bestiary of
antagonists parallel to the Werewolf corebook's Banes chapter, framed
around **the Centipede** — hengeyokai slang for the Wyrm or its
servants, "probably inspired by battles with the noisome Mukade" — and
its "Asian minions," explicitly described as no more exhaustive a list
than the Banes in the core Werewolf book, just enough to seed a
Storyteller's imagination. Named threats include Face Jackets (subject
to a Courts-mandated kill-on-sight policy) and spirits born from the
despair of drowning victims that lure travelers to their deaths. Also
addresses Kuei-jin contact directly: hengeyokai (with the exception of
Kumo, who answer to "their dark patrons" instead) are explicitly barred
by their bond to the Emerald Mother from the kind of soul-corruption
that produces a Kuei-jin, and the text states flatly that no
shapeshifter has ever been reborn as a Kuei-jin, while noting rumors of
a few hengeyokai who've still tried to find a loophole.
**Key themes:** parallel antagonist structure to core Werewolf (a
Wyrm-lord analogue, a Bane-analogue bestiary) built from distinctly
Asian folklore rather than reskinned Western monsters; a firm textual
line drawn between hengeyokai and Kuei-jin as separate fates.
**Key terms/entities:** the Centipede, Mukade, Face Jackets, Kuei-jin
(as antagonists/rivals).

## Chapter Five: The Nine-Tails (pp. 108–110)
**Summary:** A short introductory chapter to the Kitsune, opening with
a mythic framing of Gaia surveying her scattered, war-diminished
children and the foxes among them. Includes an aside (p. 109)
explaining that this Kitsune material was originally conceived as its
own standalone Changing Breed Book before being appended to
*Hengeyokai* instead — which is why Chapters Five through Eight plus
the Appendix function as a near-complete, self-contained treatment
rather than a short entry to match the other seven races' few pages
each. Includes an extensive Kitsune-specific glossary (Kanji,
Kataribe [a Kitsune Bard, one of the Four Paths], Batsu, Byakko, Chie
[Wisdom], Doshi [a Kitsune Sorcerer, one of the Four Paths], Eji [a
Kitsune Warrior, one of the Four Paths], Genko, Go-en, Gukutsushi [an
illusionist/Dreamweaver, one of the Four Paths]).
**Key themes:** the Kitsune as Gaia's youngest and most vulnerable
children; this section's own self-aware framing as an appended
mini-book.
**Key terms/entities:** the Four Paths (Kataribe, Doshi, Eji,
Gukutsushi), Kitsune-go (their own written/spoken argot).

## Chapter Six: Birth of the Youngest (pp. 111–127)
**Summary:** Origin myths and history of the Kitsune, delivered largely
through a frame story of an elder (Uncle Hu) telling tales to a young
Kitsune, Mei-Fei. Covers the Kitsune's late arrival among Gaia's
children (hence "the Youngest"), their relationship to the Wan Xian
(a recurring named group tied to Kuei-jin history elsewhere in the
book) and to spirits like Jianhua Hu and Sondok-Dukssong. Establishes
the emotional register of Kitsune lore: playful and trickster-toned on
the surface (per the Chapter Five framing) but carrying real grief and
loss underneath, consistent with the rest of the hengeyokai's
war-diminished condition.
**Key themes:** origin-through-storytelling as a structural device
(mirroring how hengeyokai culture itself transmits history orally);
the Kitsune's youth relative to other Changing Breeds as both a
strength (fewer entrenched grudges) and a vulnerability.
**Key terms/entities:** Mei-Fei Quan, Uncle Hu, Wan Xian.

## Chapter Seven: The Courtly Dance (pp. 128–136)
**Summary:** Covers Kitsune society in Asia and, per the chapter's ToC
blurb, "around the world" — how Kitsune interact with the wider Beast
Courts and with Western Garou packs and courts (a "Long Life and
Laughter" section depicts Kitsune ambassadors, the twins Matsuko
Sun-Devil and Katsuko Moon-Saint, addressing a Western Garou moot,
navigating Garou suspicion of "gaijin" outsiders). Covers Kitsune-
specific Background mechanics such as Sempai (their Mentor-equivalent,
which can be an "aunt," "uncle," birth parent, or even a whole
patronizing sentai) and social gathering customs (a "Gatherings"
section on how Kitsune numbers and etiquette work at courtly
functions).
**Key themes:** Kitsune as diplomats/go-betweens within the wider Beast
Courts and with Western Garou; the tension between Kitsune wit/
laughter and the real stakes of the political game they navigate.
**Key terms/entities:** Sempai, gaijin, Matsuko Sun-Devil, Katsuko
Moon-Saint.

## Chapter Eight: Opening of the Eyes (pp. 137–156)
**Summary:** Kitsune character creation, structured in the same
five-step process as core Werewolf chargen (Concept, Attributes,
Abilities, Advantages, Finishing Touches) but with Kitsune-specific
options at each step — initial Gnosis and Rage ranges different from
standard Garou, Path selection (the Four Paths introduced in Chapter
Five), and Kitsune-specific trait blocks (Sambuhenge, Koto, Juko, Kyubi
appear as character-sheet fields, tied to a Kitsune's tail-count/rank
progression). Also details Kitsune Renown, tracked by Rank (from
Nogitsune at Rank 0 up through Yakan/Koryo/Reiko/Tenko) across three
Renown categories analogous to but distinct from Garou's — Chie
(Wisdom-analogous), Toku, and Kagayaki (Glory-analogous) — with their
own Experience Point cost chart for Attributes, Abilities, Gifts, Rage,
Gnosis, and Willpower.
**Key themes:** deliberate mechanical distinctiveness from standard
Garou chargen despite a parallel structure — Kitsune Rank/Renown
categories are named and weighted differently, not just Garou's system
relabeled.
**Key terms/entities:** Sambuhenge, Kyubi, Nogitsune, Chie, Toku,
Kagayaki, the Rank ladder (Yakan/Koryo/Reiko/Tenko).

## Appendix: Nobles Among Nobles (pp. 157–174)
**Summary:** Pregenerated Kitsune character templates and notable
figures ("heroes"), opening with a framing fiction scene (Mei-Fei's
formal test/rite before Katsuko and her father). Includes several
named archetype writeups with quotes, Prelude, Concept, and Roleplaying
Hints sections (an "Underground Shaman" template is one example: a
wandering folk-healer/spirit-talker archetype who moves among the
human poor). The remainder of the Appendix (pp. 159 onward) is blank
character sheet forms — a hengeyokai/sentai character sheet, a
non-regenerating (mortal/Kinfolk-style) variant, and Kitsune-specific
sheet fields.
**Key themes:** capstone character options after two chapters of
Kitsune-specific rules; archetypes meant to be dropped into a
chronicle directly rather than built from scratch.
**Key terms/entities:** pregenerated templates, character sheets.

## Cross-cutting themes

**Parallel-but-distinct structure to core Werewolf.** Nearly every
core-book system gets a hengeyokai-flavored counterpart introduced by
name in the Introduction's glossary and then elaborated chapter by
chapter: auspices→Fist/Mirror/Pillar/Lantern/Leaf, Umbra→the Tapestry,
Gauntlet→the Wall, caern→Dragon Nest, pack→sentai, Wyrm-lord→the
Centipede. This is a deliberate translation layer, not a simple
reskin — several of these (the Wheel of Ages' cyclical rather than
linear cosmology; the War of Shame instead of the War of Rage; Kumo's
universal Wyrm-service) mark real structural differences from Western
Garou assumptions, not just different names for the same thing.

**The Kuei-jin as a constant offstage presence.** From the glossary's
side-by-side definition of Kin-jin/Kuei-jin/Gaki, through Chapter
Four's explicit statement that hengeyokai cannot become Kuei-jin, to
the Wan Xian's recurring role in Kitsune history (Chapter Six), the
book keeps the Kindred of the East's vampires in view as a rival
supernatural population throughout — without ever becoming a *Kindred
of the East*-line book itself. See the "Important" note at the top of
this file for how this affects cross-book/cross-edition consistency.

**The Kitsune's outsized share of the book.** Structurally, seven
hengeyokai races share Chapter Three (about 39 pages), while the
Kitsune alone get four full chapters plus the Appendix (about 67
pages) — a direct result of the material's origin as a separate,
unfinished Changing Breed Book (acknowledged explicitly on p. 109)
rather than a claim that Kitsune are more central to hengeyokai society
than the other races.
