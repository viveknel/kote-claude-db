# The Thousand Hells — Index

## How to use this file
This is a thematic index of *The Thousand Hells* (White Wolf, 1999, by Kraig Blackwelder, Tim Clancy, Geoffrey C. Grabowski and Lindsay Woodcock, with Jack Norris and Richard E. Dansky), a *Kindred of the East* supplement detailing Yomi Wan, the underworld every Kuei-jin passes through before rebirth. For exact quotes, precise rules numbers, or specific named NPCs, query `book_chunks.db` (see README.md) rather than relying on the summaries below.

## Overview
Where *Kindred of the East*'s core rulebook (Ch.1 and Ch.6) only sketches Yomi/the Thousand Hells as a nightmare-realm every Kuei-jin's P'o passes through, this book expands it into a fully detailed setting: its cosmological origin and internal geography and travel-routes (Ch.1), a "map" of its most notable named hell-domains (Ch.2), profiles of the Yama Kings who rule those domains (Ch.3), Storytelling guidance for running scenes set in Yomi (Ch.4), game systems for how Yomi warps ordinary Kuei-jin abilities (Ch.5), and a full appendix on akuma — the Kuei-jin who serve the Yama Kings — including the mechanics and cost of striking a demonic pact. The book is explicitly *not* a comprehensive gazetteer ("there are just too darn many" hells) but a guide to the most notable regions, routes, and inhabitants, written to preserve rather than dispel the horror of the setting.

## Descent of a Soul: A Cautionary Tale (pp. 5-8)
**Summary:** A fiction opener following an unnamed Kuei-jin's escape attempt through the Hell of the Seven Burning Seas, encountering the ghost ship *Keshi* (cursed to endlessly rescue wrongfully-damned souls) before diving to the ocean floor in search of a passage out.
**Key terms/entities:** Hell of the Seven Burning Seas; the *Keshi* (ghost ship); Empress of Pearls (mentioned as ruler of the ocean floor).

## Introduction (pp. 9-11)
**Summary:** Frames Yomi/the Thousand Hells/Yomi Wan as the place every Kuei-jin comes from and where the Yama Kings dwell, each awaiting a chance at the Demon Emperor's throne. Explicitly states the book is not comprehensive (too many hells to cover) but a guide to the most notable regions, routes, inhabitants, and how Yomi warps vampiric powers. Gives a terminology guide (Yama King vs. Yama Queen; akuma as those with *formal binding contracts*, as distinct from loosely-used insult; capitalization convention for "the Thousand Hells" vs. lowercase "hell"/"domain"; "soul" as synonymous with P'o in this book) and clarifies the relationship between Yomi and Ti Yu (the Earth Prison/Dark Kingdom of Jade's separate, artificial hell built by the wraith-ruler Yu Huang) — the two are different places that happen to share some inhabitants and thematic territory.
**Key themes:** Yomi as horror-genre setting to be preserved, not statted into mundanity; careful terminological distinctions.
**Key terms/entities:** Yomi Wan/Thousand Hells; akuma (strict definition); Ti Yu/Earth Prison (Yu Huang's separate hell, cross-referenced to *Dark Kingdom of Jade*).

## Chapter One: The Tapestry of Yomi (pp. 12-25)
**Summary:** Covers Yomi's cosmological history and internal travel routes/hazards. Explains how Yomi has grown over time through "annexation" of corrupted/scarred regions of the Yin and Yang Worlds (the Wicked City is cited as the most aggressive expander), and how "scars" — flaws in reality's fabric — mark newly forming hell-territory. Details major hazards for travelers: **yangstones** (addictive Yang-charged gems found in the Goblin Jungle that create dangerous illusory Chi highs, but can restore lost Appearance to ch'ing shih); the **Scarlet Path** (a jungle route through the Goblin Jungle, home to goblins, monstrous insects, sap-dripping trees, and napalm-spitting phoenixes — especially lethal to Yin-aspected travelers, particularly hard on Song of the Shadow Dharma followers); and the **Ebon Road** (the sole direct connection between Yomi and the Yin World, a flickering, nearly-unfindable black path that saps Chi from any traveler who slips off it, patrolled by Spectres and the terrifying, faceless **Unfaced Ones**, and ending at the Pit of Salt and Iron/Hell of Ti-tsang Wang).
**Key themes:** Yomi as an ever-growing, actively hostile geography; travel between worlds as a perilous act with real mechanical costs.
**Key terms/entities:** Yangstones (p.22); Scarlet Path/Goblin Jungle (p.22-23); Ebon Road (p.23-24); Unfaced Ones (p.24); Pit of Salt and Iron/Wu Hua (p.24).

## Chapter Two: The Map of Damnation: Geography (pp. 26-45)
**Summary:** The core "gazetteer" chapter, profiling the Thousand Hells' most notable named domains, each with a ruler, places of note, and denizens. Domains covered: **Kakuri** (ruled by Emma-o; home to the rebel monk-spirit Jizo the Wanderer; places of note: Kyuden no Kakuri, the Cave of Maidens); a rakshas-dominated realm ruled by **Ravana** (places of note: the Temple of Lanka, the Demon Keep of Ravana guarded by Thratri, the Jungle Path); the **Hell of Being Skinned Alive** (ruled by Tou Mu; places of note: the Silent/Sinister Stronghold of Tou Mu, the Valley of the Last Stand); the **Wicked City** (ruled by Mikaboshi; places of note: Mikaboshi Tower, the Shinjuku Necropolis/Undercity); the **Hell of Boiling Oil** (places of note: the Pockets); the **Hell of Burrowing Maggots** (ruled by Rangda; places of note: the Great Beast, the Spawning Pit); the **Pit of Salt and Iron** (ruled by Wu Hua; places of note: the Citadel of Wu Hua, the Iron Brothel); the **Hell of the Seven Burning Seas** (ruled by a Yama Queen; places of note: the Great Trench, the *Keshi*); and the **Hell of Upside-Down Sinners** (places of note: the Crossroads, with advice on "Surviving the Madness" for Kuei-jin who linger there).
**Key themes:** Geography as characterization — each hell's landscape reflects its ruler's nature and preferred cruelty.
**Key terms/entities:** Kakuri/Emma-o; rakshas domain/Ravana/Thratri; Hell of Being Skinned Alive/Tou Mu; Wicked City/Mikaboshi; Hell of Boiling Oil; Hell of Burrowing Maggots/Rangda; Pit of Salt and Iron/Wu Hua; Hell of the Seven Burning Seas; Hell of Upside-Down Sinners.

## Chapter Three: The Face of Yomi: The Yama Kings (pp. 46-63)
**Summary:** Full profiles of the Thousand Hells' ruling demon-lords. Covers the general nature and uncertain total number of Yama Kings (estimates range from a handful of "prominent" ones to thousands, one per hell), and the fact that Yama Kings can be created and destroyed. Profiles: **Emma-o** ("Daimyo of the Dark," originally an Ainu guide-of-the-dead spirit corrupted by conquest and worship shift to the Japanese; rules Kakuri; opposed within his own domain by the enigmatic monk-spirit Jizo the Wanderer, the "Honorable One"; commands an all-female akuma legion of **shikome**, whose signature power drains Willpower from men through supernatural allure). **Mikaboshi** ("Lord of the Wicked City"; a former underdog now ascendant through rapid adaptation to modern technology, now rivaling or exceeding Emma-o in soul-count and considered a leading candidate for Demon Emperor). **Ravana** (lord of the rakshas-realm, scheming to provoke a nuclear exchange between India, Pakistan, and China to harvest devastated dragon lines and mass-tainted souls — explicitly the most "current events"-flavored Yama King). **Rangda** ("Mistress of Pestilence," appears as a beautiful but disease-ridden woman; uses plague zombies and maggot-spawn to spread disease, and has taken an early interest in modern bio/chem/nuclear warfare as a soul-harvesting tool). **Tou Mu** (the "Iron Empress," ruler of the Hell of Being Skinned Alive, created by the Scarlet Queen and Ebon Dragon as a punisher of Wan Xian arrogance who became consumed by the very pride she was meant to punish; wears a sentient, shape-shifting suit of living iron armor, the **Iron Panoply**). **Yen-Lo** ("First of the Fallen") — a foundational myth explaining that Yomi grew outward from Yen-Lo's original, genuinely just realm of karmic punishment; his fellow Yama Kings (led by Emma-o and Rangda) assassinated him for his "obstinate adherence to outmoded ideals of honor," but his power passed instead to a Buddhist saint, Ama Odashu, denying Emma-o the conquest.
**Key themes:** Each Yama King as a distinct, motivated antagonist rather than an abstract evil; real-world atrocity (colonialism, WWII, nuclear war, epidemic disease) as the thematic engine behind specific Yama Kings' portfolios.
**Key terms/entities:** Emma-o/Kakuri/shikome/Jizo the Wanderer (p.51-53); Mikaboshi/Wicked City (p.53-54); Ravana/rakshas (p.54-55); Rangda (p.55-56); Tou Mu/Iron Panoply (p.56-57); Yen-Lo/Ama Odashu (p.58-59).

## Chapter Four: Storytelling in Yomi (pp. 64-79)
**Summary:** GM-facing guidance on running scenes set in the Thousand Hells. Warns against two failure modes: escalating horror into "cartoonish grotesquerie," and letting Yomi's treasures/power unbalance a chronicle. Covers antagonists and conflict design, "how to use Hell" vs. "how not to," and special procedure for running preludes where a player temporarily controls their own character's P'o ("Shadowguiding"). Gives a menu of sample plot types for Yomi-set stories: Vengeance, Rescue (incl. "Freeing the Caged Soul" for wrongfully-damned prisoners), Theft, Enlightenment, Exploration, Damnation, Diplomacy, Duty, and Punishment. Closes with advice on portraying "Denizens of Hell" — tortured souls (with a caution against glossing over their suffering, and guidance on limited, constrained interaction with them), demon servitors, and akuma (including akuma-creation guidance and the "who and how much" question of what makes a believable akuma character).
**Key themes:** Balancing horror-genre integrity against playability; treating tortured souls as more than scenery.
**Key terms/entities:** Shadowguiding (P'o preludes); sample plot types (Vengeance, Rescue, Theft, Enlightenment, Exploration, Damnation, Diplomacy, Duty, Punishment).

## Chapter Five: Systems of Yomi (pp. 80-100)
**Summary:** Game-mechanical chapter on how Yomi alters ordinary Kuei-jin existence. Covers gathering Chi in Yomi (including the "Drinking from the Same Well" rule — dragon lines in Yomi burn out and can't be revisited safely) and Demon Chi; "aspected hells" and their effect on meditation difficulty (Yin-aspected hells in particular disrupt concentration); modifications to the Chi Arts (Equilibrium, Tapestry, Yin/Yang Prana), Demon Arts (Black Wind), and Shintai/Soul Disciplines (Demon Shintai, Blood/Bone/Jade/Flesh/Ghost-Flame Shintai, Cultivation, Chi'iu Muh, Internalize, Obligation) when used in Yomi; a note that Hun/P'o balance is far harder to maintain in Yomi, since the environment constantly reinforces the P'o and makes it harder for the Hun to reassert control; ritual mechanics for escape (the Rite of Wind and Water, Behold the Spirit's Doorway, the Rite of Supplication); and a brief, rare treatment of Westerners (Kindred) in Yomi, noting how poorly documented Yomi's effects on Western Disciplines are due to the extreme rarity of Kin-jin visitors.
**Key themes:** Mechanical codification of Yomi's corrosive effect on identity and self-control.
**Key terms/entities:** Drinking from the Same Well (p.83); aspected hells (p.84); Rite of Wind and Water, Behold the Spirit's Doorway, Rite of Supplication (p.85-86); Westerners in Yomi (p.86-87).

## Appendix: Akuma: The Devil-Eaten (pp. 101-119)
**Summary:** Full rules and lore for akuma. Covers how the Yama Kings protect their favored akuma in Yomi (Jade Servant of the August Personage shielding, protective talismans) and details on P'o souls generally. **"The Economics of Damnation"** explains the "nature of the crime" that condemns a soul to Yomi, and draws a sharp distinction between **infernalism** (worship of/servitude to a higher demonic power — a capital offense among Kuei-jin) and **demonology** (the summoning and mastery of lesser demons — legal, and a specialty of the Devil-Tiger Dharma). Details the ritual mechanics of striking a pact: the **Black Peony Chant** (a multi-hour ritual of unnatural mantras and worsening nightmares that culminates in a dream-audience with a Yama King) and "Iris Bulb Commerce" as alternate contact methods, plus "hidden costs" (Yama Kings extracting minor services, memories, or emotional capacity even from akuma who think they've walked away clean) and "Dream Visitations and Courtly Games." Lists sample **Treatments** — specific supernatural gifts/mutations Yama Kings grant their akuma servants — including the Crimson Tiger-general Treatment, Pearl Teeth and Amber Eyes Treatment, Beatifically Enlightened Tutor Treatment, Saint of the Godbody Treatment, the Artful Clockwork Prince Treatment (Mikaboshi's signature), and the Seven Wisdoms Minister Treatment. Closes with "Legends of the Devil-Eaten," profiling named akuma NPCs: the Black Animal Generals; **Tashimoto Hanko**, a legendary akuma who stole his own soul-vase from Emma-o's treasury and fled along the Ebon Road, prompting an island-wide manhunt; and **Miriam Weatherbee**, a Kindred masquerading among Kuei-jin, entangled with Penangallan infernalists in the Golden Courts (a direct cross-reference to the Companion's Ch.1 Golden Courts material).
**Key themes:** Damnation as a negotiated, transactional relationship rather than simple corruption; the porous, cross-book connective tissue between this appendix and the Companion's Golden Courts/Penangallan material.
**Key terms/entities:** Infernalism vs. demonology (p.109); Black Peony Chant (p.110-111); Iris Bulb Commerce; Treatments (Crimson Tiger-general, Pearl Teeth and Amber Eyes, Beatifically Enlightened Tutor, Saint of the Godbody, Artful Clockwork Prince, Seven Wisdoms Minister); Tashimoto Hanko; Miriam Weatherbee.

## Cross-cutting themes
- **Yomi as the physical realization of the core book's cosmology**: directly expands *Kindred of the East* Ch.1 (the soul's journey through Yomi before the Second Breath) and Ch.6 (the brief Yomi Realms section — Wicked City/Mikaboshi, Kakuri/Emma-o, Hell of Being Skinned Alive/Tou Mu are all named there and given full chapters here).
- **Named Yama Kings recur across the library**: Rangda (also detailed in the Companion's Golden Courts material as an antagonist of the Penangallan) and the general Yama King roster (Mikaboshi, Emma-o, Tou Mu) are consistent across the core book, Companion, and this volume.
- **Akuma as a recurring, deepening topic**: the core book introduces akuma briefly; the Companion (Ch.2) adds sociological/political nuance; this book's appendix gives the fullest treatment — the pact mechanics, Treatments, and named NPC akuma.
- **Infernalism vs. demonology**: a legal/doctrinal distinction unique to this book that clarifies why the Devil-Tiger Dharma (core book Ch.2) can practice demon-summoning without being akuma themselves.
