# World of Darkness: Blood & Silk — Index

## How to use this file
This is a thematic index of *World of Darkness: Blood & Silk* (White Wolf, 2000), a historical supplement for *Kindred of the East* set at the very end of the Fourth Age (the year 1197 CE). For exact quotes, precise rules numbers, or specific named NPCs, query `book_chunks.db` (see README.md) rather than relying on the summaries below.

## Overview
Blood & Silk moves the *Kindred of the East* line backward in time roughly 800 years, to the twilight of the Fourth Age ("the Age of Beautiful Sadness") — a Southern Song/Kamakura-era Asia on the eve of the Mongol conquests. It is explicitly a *historical* companion rather than a replacement for the core rulebook: where material is unchanged from the Fifth Age (modern) setting, the book doesn't repeat it. The book covers Fourth Age history and cosmology (Ch.1), Dharmas as understood in this earlier era (Ch.2), period-appropriate character creation (Ch.3), an extensive set of new Traits and Disciplines — most notably the elemental "Godbody" powers that are the historical ancestors of the Fifth Age's Shintai Disciplines — plus medieval combat systems (Ch.4), Storytelling guidance built around the Chinese dynastic cycle (Ch.5), crossover material for running Fourth Age hengeyokai (Xiong Ren), hsien, early Kindred contact, and mages (Ch.6), and a gazetteer appendix profiling five historical Wan Kuei courts/cities (Ch. Appendix). This is the deepest historical-worldbuilding book in the library and directly explains the *origins* of institutions (the Quincunx courts, the Shintai Disciplines, the Kuei-jin/Kindred relationship) that the other four books treat as already-established fact.

## Introduction (pp. 5-10)
**Summary:** Sets the scene at 1197 CE: the Southern Song dynasty ruling half of a divided China, Japan just emerging from civil war, the Mongols a few decades from erupting across Asia. Recaps the Wan Xian/Wan Kuei fall myth (as in the core book) and introduces the other shen sharing the era — Xiong Ren ("the Ferocious People," pre-Hengeyokai terminology), hsien, chi'n ta, and the Yama Kings' agents (Kumo Goblin Spiders, Kura Sau). Frames the era as "the turning Age" — the Fourth Age's end approaching, with young and old Wan Kuei alike anxious about what comes next. Explains the book's function as a historical companion assuming *Kindred of the East* core-rules familiarity.
**Key themes:** A civilization at a hinge point, mirroring the core book's Fifth Age anxiety one full Age earlier.
**Key terms/entities:** Fourth Age/Age of Beautiful Sadness; Xiong Ren (period term for hengeyokai); Wan Kuei (period-appropriate self-designation, predating "Kuei-jin").

## Chapter One: Hungry Dead of the Middle Kingdom (World) (pp. 11-47)
**Summary:** The largest worldbuilding chapter, framed partly as an in-character treatise by "Lu Feng, South Wind of the Flitting Hummingbird Wu" (later "hin in the Court of Linan"). Covers **The Fallen** (what Wan Kuei are, wu and court structure, the Dharmas including a note on heretical paths), **What We Were** (a full retelling of Wan Xian history, including the tragic love of Wang Yuanfeng and a fall-myth naming one of the four fallen ancestors, **Bao Zhong**, who became a Yama King and founded his own hell), **What Will Be** (prophecy of the coming Fifth Age/Sixth Age, the seeds of the "Running Monkey" youth-conflict later formalized in *Shadow War*), and **The Others** (Xiong Ren, hsien, Yama Kings, and a full taxonomy of **akuma types** — the "Reluctant," "Aggressive," and a third, most dangerous "hidden/political" type). A second treatise section, **The History of the Middle Kingdom**, retells Chinese dynastic history from the Wan Kuei's perspective across the First through Fourth Ages: the Three Sovereigns (Fuxi, Shennong, Huang-ti) and the Xia Kingdom (Yu the Great); the Shang dynasty and the archer-hero Yi; the Zhou dynasty (Spring and Autumn period, Laotze); the Qin Empire (First Emperor Qin Shihuangdi's book-burning purge of Wan Kuei texts, and his afterlife as the Jade/Dead Emperor Yu Huang, ruler of the Yellow Springs — directly cross-referencing *Dark Kingdom of Jade*); the Han Empire; the Three Kingdoms; the Sui dynasty; the Tang dynasty (Empress Wu, the An Lushan rebellion); the Five Dynasties/Ten Kingdoms period; the Song dynasty; and the Southern Song/Jin Empire split that defines the book's "present." A long in-character spy report ("Life in Southern Song China," from Amrat Atansas to a foreign king) details daily life, food and drink, family relations, arts and leisure, the five elements calendar, war (gunpowder), and labor. A companion report ("Beyond the Empire") covers Xixia and Jin, Nihon/Japan (the Genpei War between the Taira and Minamoto clans, Minamoto-no-Yoritomo's rise to shogun, the sinking of the imperial regalia), and the Jews of Kaifeng. The chapter closes with a tour of notable Yin World and Yang World spirit-realms accessible in this era: the Home of the Final Blossom, the Shards of the Imperial Libraries, the Watery Kingdom of Fu Yi (a drowned kingdom now a ghost-realm), the Realm of the Celestial Blacksmith (Ru Shou), and the Kingdom of Fiery Passions.
**Key themes:** History-as-cosmology; the Wan Kuei as unseen participants shaping "real" dynastic history; direct textual ancestry for institutions used throughout the rest of the library.
**Key terms/entities:** Bao Zhong (fallen ancestor turned Yama King); Qin Shihuangdi/Yu Huang (cross-ref *Dark Kingdom of Jade*); the Genpei War/Minamoto-no-Yoritomo; Fu Yi (drowned kingdom); Ru Shou (Celestial Blacksmith); akuma taxonomy (Reluctant/Aggressive/Hidden).

## Chapter Two: Many Roads Back (Dharmas) (pp. 48-61)
**Summary:** Revisits the Fivefold Way/Great Principle and the five Dharmas as understood and practiced in the Fourth Age — before the Quincunx's modern bureaucratic form solidified, when the Court of the Azure Dragon (later collapsed, per the core book and Companion) was still a going concern. Includes a **Fourth Age Dharma Chart** with period-appropriate status/age benchmarks (generally lower ceilings than the modern chart, since fewer centuries have passed). Also revisits the heretical Dharmas in brief ("Heresy in Brief"), noting some (like Spirit of the Living Earth) are not yet available/practiced in this era.
**Key themes:** Institutional youth — Dharmas and courts shown before their Fifth Age codification.
**Key terms/entities:** Fourth Age Dharma Chart; Court of the Azure Dragon (still extant, pre-collapse).

## Chapter Three: Taking the Second Breath (Characters) (pp. 62-81)
**Summary:** Character creation rules for Fourth Age Wan Kuei, built as a delta against the core rulebook's modern chargen rather than a full restatement. Walks through choosing balance, direction, Dharma, Hun/P'o Natures, and guanxi exactly as in the modern game, but flags era-specific differences: Fourth Age-appropriate Abilities, a Discipline-purchase rule requiring two of five starting dots go into Demon Arts, and notes on which Disciplines exist yet in this era (Ghost-Flame Shintai is unknown in the Fourth Age; Beast, Smoke, and Storm Shintai already exist). Gives "From Numbers to Wan Kuei Concepts" — narrative guidance for fleshing out a period-appropriate character concept.
**Key themes:** Explicit period-accuracy bookkeeping — which modern-book Disciplines/Traits are anachronistic in 1197 CE.
**Key terms/entities:** Fourth Age Discipline-availability rules (Ghost-Flame Shintai absent; Beast/Smoke/Storm present).

## Chapter Four: Ways Under Heaven (Traits and Systems) (pp. 82-125)
**Summary:** The largest systems chapter. Introduces new **P'o Natures** for the era (the Ancient, the Artist, the Echo, the Sage, the Vitalist, the Eunuch, the Warrior), new Skills (Archery, Meditation, Pilot, Sailing, Ride), and a new Merits/Flaws system built around **Favors** (a social-debt economy). The chapter's centerpiece is the **Godbody Disciplines** — Godbody of Earth, Fire, Metal, Water, and Wood — elemental transformation powers that are the direct historical ancestors of the Fifth Age's Blood/Bone/Jade/Flesh Shintai Disciplines (each with detailed power lists, e.g. Godbody of Fire's "Fire in the Eye/Hand," "Draining the Fire," "the Tempered Soul"; Godbody of Water's "Command of Flow in Self/Others," "the Water Lash," "the Soul Tide"; Godbody of Wood's "Pruning the Self," "Transplanting the Self," "the Vital Command," "the Spark of Life"). Closes with an extensive medieval combat/equipment system: parrying items (shields, war fans, wind-fire wheels, sai, tonfa), mounted combat (trampling, lances, mounted bows), period martial arts, and special weapons (repeating crossbows, flamethrowers, black-powder rockets).
**Key themes:** Mechanical genealogy — showing the Fifth Age's signature Disciplines as evolved forms of these Fourth Age Godbody powers; grounding the setting in real medieval Chinese military technology.
**Key terms/entities:** Godbody of Earth/Fire/Metal/Water/Wood (pp. 83ff); Favors (Merits/Flaws system); mounted combat and special weapons (repeating crossbows, flamethrowers, rockets).

## Chapter Five: Tales of Silk (Storytelling) (pp. 126-133)
**Summary:** Storytelling guidance built around the Chinese **dynastic cycle** as a narrative/thematic structure mapped onto the direction system: Birth in Virtue (south), Growth in Prosperity (east), Mature Success (west), and by implication further steps for north and center. Covers "Facing the Dynastic Way" (Storyteller advice for running petition/patronage scenes), then general period-chronicle advice: setting the scene, "the foundations" of Asian society, cities and villages, and farms/agricultural cycles as sources of story hooks. Closes with notes on long-term play (downtime handling, Dharma-roll pacing) and "notes on crossing over" — running mixed troupes with Xiong Ren, hsien, and other shen.
**Key themes:** The dynastic cycle as both historical fact and Storytelling structure; downtime/long-campaign pacing advice.
**Key terms/entities:** Dynastic cycle mapped to direction (south/east/west/north/center); downtime Dharma-roll pacing guidance.

## Chapter Six: Barbarians and Shen (Crossovers) (pp. 134-159)
**Summary:** Crossover chapter for running other *World of Darkness* game lines in the Fourth Age Middle Kingdom. Gives a full roster of **Xiong Ren** ("Ferocious People," pre-Hengeyokai) breeds with Fourth Age framing: Hakken (wolves), Stargazer, Khan (weretigers), Kitsune (werefoxes, a newer breed born from the Age's chaos, serving as diplomats for the Beast Courts of the Emerald Mother), Kumo (weretspiders/Goblin Spiders, villainous), Nagah (wereserpents), Nezumi (wererats), Same-Bito (weresharks, bound by the Code of Sensei Mizuchi), Tengu (wereravens), and Zhong Lung (weredragons, the eldest and most scholarly breed) — with sample named Beast Court characters (Honganji Mito, Ju Jasperclaw, Tong Long Hua). Covers the **hsien** (Asian fae) — their history as agents of the August Personage of Jade stranded in the Middle Kingdom, divided into three factions, with a "Wu Tan Made Easy" conversion note tying hsien Alchemy to the Godbody Disciplines from Ch.4. Covers early Kindred (Cainite) contact — Western missionary-vampires (referencing the real historical Franciscan friar Giovanni de Plano Carpini), the antipathy and occasional scholarly interest between Wan Kuei Dharmas and Cainite clans (Ventrue, Lasombra, Toreador, Tzimisce, and a special courteous-but-cold welcome extended to the Salubri), and "Spanning the Chasm" advice for interaction. Covers **mages** — the Order of Reason's early tension with the Wu Lung (Dragon Wizards) magical tradition, foreshadowing the Order's eventual "Ministerial Collapse" subversion of the Wu Lung by the Ming dynasty's fall (17th century) — plus the Traditions (Ahl-i-Batin, Akashic Brotherhood) and a brief note on Garou/Wan Kuei relations (incl. the Silent Striders).
**Key themes:** The Fourth Age as a wide-open crossover sandbox; documenting the historical origin of later canonical conflicts (Wu Lung vs. Order of Reason's Technocracy).
**Key terms/entities:** Xiong Ren breeds (Hakken, Stargazer, Khan, Kitsune, Kumo, Nagah, Nezumi, Same-Bito, Tengu, Zhong Lung); Hsien/Wu Tan; Giovanni de Plano Carpini; Salubri; Wu Lung vs. Order of Reason.

## Appendix: Cities of the Dead (Locations) (pp. 160-174)
**Summary:** A gazetteer of five historical Wan Kuei power centers, each with named ancestors, wu, and political intrigue. **Changan** (the former imperial capital, seat of the Yellow Emperor Court under ancestor **Wang Hongben** and the Golden Jade Society; three prominent wu — Amber Wren, the Illustrious Scribes of the Yellow River, and the Bronze Pennant, led by a First Oni). **Dhillika** (Delhi, under Rajput rule following Muhammad Ghori's conquests, with the ancestor **Pariksit**). **Kamakura** (Japan, seat of shogun Minamoto-no-Yoritomo; the Bishamon wu monitors the shogun's increasingly paranoid, temple-building decline, while the secretive **Blackest Night wu**, sworn to the memory of a wronged figure named Yasutami, works from the shadows). **Kashi/Kashgar** (a Silk Road crossroads city; the ancestor **Jiang Shahua** rules through the Ministry of Gilt Diplomacy and the Talons of Jasper and Gold wu, executing any who speak too freely of Wan Kuei secrets). **P'yong'yang** (capital of the Koryo kingdom, wracked by a succession of assassinations and coups; the **Night Patrol** wu, led by the ambitious General Wang Sing-nyom, has yet to confirm whether one contender for the throne, Wang Kyu, is legitimate). Closes with a blank Kindred of the East character sheet template.
**Key themes:** Named political intrigue as a template for GMs to run era-specific court politics; explicit forerunners to the modern courts detailed in the Companion (Changan/Yellow Emperor Court directly prefigures the Quincunx's Jade Court, per Companion Ch.1).
**Key terms/entities:** Changan/Wang Hongben/Golden Jade Society; Dhillika/Pariksit; Kamakura/Minamoto-no-Yoritomo/Blackest Night wu; Kashi/Jiang Shahua; P'yong'yang/Night Patrol/Wang Sing-nyom.

## Cross-cutting themes
- **Direct historical ancestry for later-book institutions**: Changan's Yellow Emperor Court (Appendix) is the direct predecessor of the Quincunx's Jade Court (Companion Ch.1's Treaty of the Quincunx, 1304 CE — just over a century after this book's setting); the Godbody Disciplines (Ch.4) are the explicit ancestors of the Fifth Age Shintai Disciplines (core book Ch.4); Qin Shihuangdi's afterlife as Yu Huang (Ch.1) directly cross-references *The Thousand Hells*' Introduction distinction between Yomi and Ti Yu/the Yellow Springs.
- **The dynastic cycle as cosmology**: Ch.1's historical narrative and Ch.5's Storytelling framework both use the rise-flourish-decline pattern of Chinese dynasties as a structural mirror for the Wheel of Ages itself.
- **Akuma and Yama Kings before their Fifth Age forms**: Bao Zhong's origin story (Ch.1) supplies a founding myth in the same vein as *The Thousand Hells*' Yen-Lo story, suggesting the Yama King pantheon was still actively forming/being conquered in this era.
- **A wider-open, less segregated supernatural world**: Ch.6's crossover material (early Kindred contact, active Order of Reason/Wu Lung rivalry, Xiong Ren breeds not yet consolidated into "hengeyokai") shows the Middle Kingdom's supernatural ecology before the more settled, walled-off state depicted in the core rulebook and Companion.
