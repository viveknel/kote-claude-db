# Kindred of the East Companion — Index

## How to use this file
This is a thematic index of the *Kindred of the East Companion* (White Wolf, by Phyllis Bowen, Mark Cenczyk, Jess Heining, Amy Lindgren and Steve Long), a supplement expanding the *Kindred of the East* core rulebook. For exact quotes, precise rules numbers, or specific named NPCs, query `book_chunks.db` (see README.md) rather than relying on the summaries below.

## Overview
The Companion deepens the core *Kindred of the East* book across six areas: the political/historical detail of the Kuei-jin courts (Ch.1), other Chi-stealing "demon people" beyond the standard Kuei-jin — dhampyrs, body-hopping yulan-jin, and akuma — plus four heretical Dharmas (Ch.2), an extensive expansion of Disciplines/supernatural powers (Ch.3), the astrological "direction" system (Ch.4), an in-depth survey of real-world Asian religions as adapted for Kuei-jin cosmology (Ch.5), and Kuei-jin/Kindred cross-contact both in Asia and abroad (Ch.6), closing with a rules-conversion appendix for *Vampire: The Masquerade* Revised Edition compatibility. Where the core book paints Kuei-jin society in broad strokes, this volume supplies the specific courts, named ancestors, founding treaties, and doctrinal splinter groups that make the setting usable at the table.

## Introduction (pp. 3-5)
**Summary:** Frames the book as a toolbox of "advanced" material — history, mechanics, and roleplaying hooks — to be introduced sparingly so the exotic stays exotic. Previews all six chapters and the appendix; cautions Storytellers to vet new Disciplines/Dharmas/vampire types carefully before dropping them into an existing chronicle.
**Key themes:** Expansion-as-seasoning, not overhaul; GM-facing framing throughout.

## Chapter One: The Hundred Corpse Families (pp. 6-25)
**Summary:** A court-by-court history of Kuei-jin political structures across China, Japan, and the Golden Courts. Explains the fall of the original Five August Courts (Black Tortoise/north, White Tiger/west, Azure Dragon/east, Scarlet Phoenix/south, Yellow Emperor/center) and their 1304 CE replacement by the **Treaty of the Quincunx**, signed at Shaolin monastery near Changan by the surviving Black Tortoise, White Tiger, and Yellow Emperor ancestors (the "Council of Dragons"); gives the treaty's preamble and provisions verbatim, establishing the five new courts — **Blood Court** (north, seated in Beijing, the Quincunx's central authority), **Flesh Court** (east, Shanghai), **Flame Court** (south, Hong Kong), **Bone Court** (west, Chongqing), and **Jade Court** (center, Changan) — plus the rotating figurehead **Bestowed Ancestor of the Celestial Realms**. Covers Japan's Uji history: the original gaki Bishamon driven into hiding by Chinese Kuei-jin invaders (~600 BCE) who set up **House Genji** under the Azure Dragon Court; the Bishamon's return and the **Year of the Black Kites** purge that broke Genji power; the modern **House Genji** as a Meiji-era reinvention allied with Western Sabbat packs; and the **Iga** and **Koga**, originally Bishamon defense sub-uji, now independent mercenary/enforcer families. Covers the Golden Courts (Southeast Asia) in detail: the matriarchal **Penangallan** (Yang-aspected women who master Flesh Shintai, tested for akuma-taint via ritual), the akuma-plagued history under Yama Queen Rangda, the bodhisattva Piyau Payung who broke a mass-akuma uprising in an 11-night battle, **Singapore's "Golden Lion Court"** (a male-run sub-court exiled from Penangallan rule, dominated by the Resplendent Crane Dharma), and the **Infinite Thunders (Bijali) Court** of Sri Lanka/eastern India, center of the heretical Flame of the Rising Phoenix Dharma and ruled by Brahmin warrior-priests. Closes with a discussion of wu formation and "breaking guanxi" (severing the social-obligation bonds within a wu).
**Key themes:** Political history as worldbuilding; the tension between centralizing Quincunx authority and independent/"barbarian" courts; matriarchy vs. patriarchy in the Golden Courts.
**Key terms/entities:** Five August Courts (Black Tortoise, White Tiger, Azure Dragon, Scarlet Phoenix, Yellow Emperor); Treaty of the Quincunx, 1304 CE (p.7); Blood/Flesh/Flame/Bone/Jade Courts; Bestowed Ancestor of the Celestial Realms (p.8); House Bishamon and House Genji (p.12-14); Year of the Black Kites; Iga and Koga (p.14); Golden Courts/Penangallan (p.18); Yama Queen Rangda; bodhisattva Piyau Payung; Singapore/Golden Lion Court (p.20); Infinite Thunders/Bijali Court (p.21).
**Notable claims/data points:** Treaty of the Quincunx explicitly bars all contact with "Children of Kain" (Kindred) and strips the Azure Dragon/Scarlet Phoenix courts of authority for refusing to sign — the origin of the Quincunx/Golden Courts rift referenced in the core book.

## Chapter Two: The Demon People (pp. 26-45)
**Summary:** Introduces three non-standard Chi-stealing beings and four heretical Dharmas. **Dhampyrs**: half-human, centuries-lived offspring of Yang-imbalanced Kuei-jin (mentioned but not detailed in the core book); raised as tools/pawns by their vampire parents, they mature into isolated "Shade Walkers," with full character-creation rules given. **Yulan-jin** ("Soul Jumpers"): Kuei-jin whose souls lack the fortitude to stay in one corpse and instead body-hop upon "death," losing a Dharma point and possibly memories each time they do; socially excluded from courts, they track each other via consistent use-names (e.g. "Yulan-Ho"). **Akuma** revisited in more sociological detail — both the "true" demon-servants of the Yama Kings and the more common political scapegoats falsely branded akuma by hostile ancestors. Presents four **heretical Dharmas** rejected by Quincunx orthodoxy: **Flame of the Rising Phoenix** (Virtues: Hun/Yang; seeks to complete one's unfinished mortal life and thereby earn release from the Cycle — centered at the Infinite Thunders Court), **Tempest of Inward Focus** (Virtue: Balance; a more ascetic/introspective rival to the Path of a Thousand Whispers, seeking stillness amid opposing forces), **The Face of Gods** (Virtues: Hun/P'o; "Godlings" who believe Kuei-jin are fallen divinities entitled to reclaim heavenly thrones via mortal cults), and **Spirit of the Living Earth** (animist/Shinto-influenced "Cerulean Veils" who seek release from the dual-soul trap through spirit appeasement and ritual). Each Dharma entry follows the core book's format (Virtue, overview, training, weakness, omens, affiliations, concepts, tenets, rival-path quotes).
**Key themes:** The porousness of the "standard Kuei-jin" category; heresy and doctrinal conflict as an organizing structure paralleling the five orthodox Dharmas.
**Key terms/entities:** Dhampyrs (p.28); Yulan-jin/Soul Jumpers (p.32); Akuma (p.35); Flame of the Rising Phoenix (p.38); Tempest of Inward Focus (p.40); The Face of Gods (p.42); Spirit of the Living Earth (p.44, "Cerulean Veils").

## Chapter Three: Ten Thousand Terrors (pp. 46-80)
**Summary:** A large Disciplines-expansion chapter, framed around change/adaptation as a Kuei-jin virtue. Expands the four Chi Arts (new powers for Equilibrium, Feng Shui, Tapestry, Yin/Yang Prana — e.g. Feng Shui's Wind-Water Eye, Broom of Heaven; Tapestry's Celestial Needle, Dragon Chariot, Chi Castle); expands Demon Arts (Black Wind's A Thousand Cuts, Divine Whirlwind) and Demon Shintai; and introduces new elemental **Chintai Disciplines** beyond the core five — **Beast Chintai** (animal-spirit powers, incl. sample Beast Spirit powers for Carp, Crane, Fox, Squid, Tiger), **Smoke Shintai**, and **Storm Shintai** (incl. Cloud Chariot, Flight of the Dragons). Expands Soul Arts (Cultivation, Internalize, Obligation) and introduces two new Soul Arts: **Mibasham** (illusion/reality-bending, e.g. Veil of Maya, Revisionist Reality) and **Tzu Wei** (astrological fate-manipulation, e.g. Realigning the Stars, Pantheon of Ancestors, Celestial Curse, Fate of the Wu/Court — includes a full directional/astrological table).
**Key themes:** Mechanical depth for elder/specialist characters; fate and astrology (Tzu Wei) as a new supernatural axis alongside Chi and soul mastery.
**Key terms/entities:** Feng Shui, Tapestry, Yin/Yang Prana (expanded); Beast Chintai, Smoke Shintai, Storm Shintai (new); Mibasham, Tzu Wei (new Soul Arts).

## Chapter Four: Wandering Direction (pp. 81-88)
**Summary:** Expands the core book's five-direction system (north/east/west/south/center) as an astrologically-determined, largely non-negotiable aspect of a Kuei-jin's identity — contrasted with Dharma, which is chosen. Explains how direction is divined at a disciple's initiation and how it governs individual, wu-level, and court-level purpose and role assignment.
**Key themes:** Fate vs. choice; direction as a second axis of identity alongside the chosen Dharma.
**Key terms/entities:** Direction (north/east/west/south/center); confluence (the moment of death/rebirth that sets a soul's destiny).

## Chapter Five: The Breath of Gods (pp. 89-108)
**Summary:** An extensive, semi-academic survey of real-world Asian and Middle Eastern religions, each given a Kuei-jin-specific gloss. **Buddhism**: the life of Prince Siddhartha, the Buddhist worldview, the Road to Nirvana, Buddhism's spread in the Middle Kingdom, modern sects (incl. the "Way of the Seven Lakes"), a Kuei-jin theory of the bodhisattva's origins, and the Eightfold Path's relevance to Kuei-jin redemption. **Confucianism**: the Four Books, its spread, the "Great Harmony," the Wise Ruler and Loving Family ideals, and the Path of Jen — noted as especially embraced by Resplendent Crane and Song of the Shadow followers. **Taoism**: Lao-Tzu's teachings, the rise of religious Taoism, Taoist alchemy, internal/external (hard/soft) practice, and the "Eight Most August Wan Xian." **Shinto**: kami cosmology, the Shinto worldview and morality, the Yomi-no-kuni (the Shinto underworld, distinct from the Kuei-jin Yomi World), and kami worship among Kuei-jin. **Hinduism**: karma, the Dharmic origin of the term "Dharma" itself, Hindu yoga and the Four Roads to Enlightenment, and Hindu polytheism ("the Many are One"). **Islam**: Islamic culture in Asia, the Five Pillars, and Kuei-jin engagement with Islamic doctrine. **Christianity**: the history of Christian missionaries in Asia, the "White Christ," and Christianity's uneasy relationship with Kuei-jin theology. Closes with **Popular Religion** — folk worship of "the Old Gods" and a "Supernatural Bureaucracy" of minor gods.
**Key themes:** Real-world religious pluralism as the backbone of Kuei-jin roleplaying flavor; each faith's compatibility (or conflict) with Dharmic cosmology.
**Key terms/entities:** Buddhism (Eightfold Path, bodhisattva origins); Confucianism (Jen, Great Harmony); Taoism (Eight Most August Wan Xian); Shinto (kami, Yomi-no-kuni); Hinduism (Dharma's etymology); Islam (Five Pillars); Christianity (the White Christ); Popular Religion/Supernatural Bureaucracy.

## Chapter Six: Gweilo (pp. 109-117)
**Summary:** Covers cross-contact between Kuei-jin and Kindred (Camarilla and Sabbat) both within Asia and abroad. Surveys Kin-jin activity and Kuei-jin countermeasures region by region — China, Japan, Korea, India, Southeast Asia — then flips to **Kuei-jin Abroad**: Cathayans operating in the Middle East (alongside Assamites and Followers of Set), Europe, and North America, including a section on "the Western Paradise." Introduces the **kanbujian** ("unable to see") — Kuei-jin who undergo the Second Breath outside the Middle Kingdom (e.g. in Chinatowns) and thus have no access to Dharmic instruction; full character rules are given (begins with Humanity 1 instead of a Dharma, cannot learn complex Disciplines like Feng Shui/Tzu Wei/the Pranas/Chi'iu Muh/Tapestry, treated as a non-person by other Kuei-jin, a 4-point Cathayan Society Flaw).
**Key themes:** Kuei-jin xenophobia toward Kindred, mirrored by the isolation of Kuei-jin themselves when displaced from the Middle Kingdom (the kanbujian).
**Key terms/entities:** Kin-jin in Asia (China/Japan/Korea/India/Southeast Asia, pp.109-114); Kuei-jin Abroad (Middle East/Europe/North America, pp.115-116); Kanbujian (p.116-117).

## Appendix: Rules of the Hungry Dead (pp. 118-127)
**Summary:** A rules-conversion appendix reconciling *Kindred of the East* with the *Vampire: The Masquerade* Revised Edition. Covers updated/moved Abilities, health/damage/dying rules for Kuei-jin (bashing vs. lethal vs. aggravated soak, Yin/Yang-aspected differences with firearms), resisting mental intrusion (Kuei-jin's dual-soul resistance via P'o rolls), and an extensive martial arts styles section (Aikido, Chin Na, Hsing-I Chuan, Karate, Pa Kua, Pencak Silat, and more, each with soft/hard classification and typical maneuvers). Also covers Discipline-learning rules and "the Powers of Jade," plus general crossover considerations for mixing Kuei-jin into standard *Vampire* chronicles.
**Key themes:** Rules-system compatibility/maintenance rather than lore.
**Key terms/entities:** Martial arts styles (Aikido, Chin Na, Hsing-I Chuan, Karate, Pa Kua, Pencak Silat); soak rules; Powers of Jade.

## Cross-cutting themes
- **The Quincunx's founding and internal politics** (Ch.1's Treaty of the Quincunx, the five new courts, the Bestowed Ancestor) supplies the concrete history that the core rulebook's Ch.1 and Ch.6 only gesture at — read together, these fill out the Quincunx's full structure.
- **Heresy and doctrinal splintering**: Ch.2's four heretical Dharmas mirror the core book's five orthodox ones in structure, extending the "Dharma as opposed philosophy" theme from *Kindred of the East* Ch.2.
- **Exile and displacement**: recurs from the Golden Courts' "barbarian" status (Ch.1) through the yulan-jin's social exclusion (Ch.2) to the kanbujian's total isolation from Dharma (Ch.6) — Kuei-jin society repeatedly defines itself by casting out the irregular.
- **Real-world religion as cosmological infrastructure**: Ch.5's deep dive complements and grounds the core book's brief "Great Faiths of the East" section (Ch.2 of the core book).
