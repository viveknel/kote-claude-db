# Kindred of the East — Index

## How to use this file
This is a thematic index of the *Kindred of the East* core rulebook (White Wolf, 1998), the foundational sourcebook for the Kuei-jin (Asian vampires) line for *Vampire: The Masquerade*. For exact quotes, precise rules numbers, or specific named NPCs/artifacts, query `book_chunks.db` (see README.md in this bundle) rather than relying on the summaries below.

## Overview
*Kindred of the East* introduces the Kuei-jin (also called Wan Kuei, "Ten Thousand Demons," or Cathayans), a species of Asian undead entirely separate from the Kindred/Cainites of core *Vampire: The Masquerade*. Where Kindred descend from Caine, Kuei-jin are reincarnated mortal souls sent back from the underworld (the Yomi World) to atone for karmic failings; their curse is tied to a dual soul (Hun, the higher/rational soul, and P'o, the demonic/animal soul) and to the cosmic Wheel of Ages rather than to a bloodline. The book covers Kuei-jin cosmology and society (Ch. 1), the five spiritual paths or Dharmas that structure Kuei-jin unlife (Ch. 2), character creation (Ch. 3), the Discipline system ("Shintai" and other Chi-based arts, Ch. 4), further game systems for Chi, combat, and travel (Ch. 5), the setting — the "Middle Kingdom" of Asia and its associated spirit worlds (Ch. 6), Storytelling guidance (Ch. 7), and other supernatural beings/rivals the Kuei-jin share Asia with (Ch. 8). The book's throughline is balance vs. imbalance — cosmically, socially, and within each vampire's divided soul — set against the backdrop of an approaching apocalypse (the Sixth Age).

## Introduction: Behind the Wall (pp. 4-17)
**Summary:** Frames the "Middle Kingdom" (supernatural Asia) as a world unreachable by Western Kindred, ruled by an entirely different species of vampire. Introduces the Yin and Yang Worlds (the spirit realms flanking the Middle Kingdom, separated from it by the Wall), the Wheel of Ages/Great Cycle (a 12-age cosmic cycle currently in its Fifth Age, the "Age of Darkness," heading toward the apocalyptic Sixth Age, the "Age of Sorrow"), and Chi (the universal life-energy that Kuei-jin must steal from others, paralleling Kindred's need for blood). Lays out core Kuei-jin/Kindred distinctions point by point (no clans/generation, no Blood Bond via Kuei-jin blood, Dharmas instead of Humanity, aggravated damage from fire/claws, rot rather than burn in sunlight, etc.) and closes with an extensive lexicon of Kuei-jin terminology plus a "suggested resources" media list.
**Key themes:** East vs. West cosmology; balance/imbalance; approaching apocalypse (Sixth Age/Gehenna parallel).
**Key terms/entities:** Middle Kingdom (p.7), Shen (p.7), Yin/Yang Worlds (p.7), Wheel of Ages/Great Cycle (p.8), Fifth Age/Age of Darkness, Sixth Age/Age of Sorrow (p.8-9), Chi (p.9), Kuei-jin/Wan Kuei/Gui Ren/Cathayan (p.9-10), Second Breath (p.10), Hun and P'o (p.11), Dharma (p.11), extensive lexicon (pp.13-16: Ancestor, Bodhisattva, Court, Dragon Nest, Dragon Line, Kin-jin, Wu, Yama Kings, Yomi World, etc.)
**Notable claims/data points:** Kuei-jin population ratio implied similar to Kindred (later given as ~1 per 100,000 mortals, Ch.1); eight-chapter structure explained; author's note that "vampire" in the book means Kuei-jin unless stated otherwise.

## Chapter One: The Hungry Dead (pp. 18-45)
**Summary:** The society chapter. Covers dragon nests (Chi-rich sites where the Wall is weak) and dragon tracks/lines connecting them; how a mortal becomes Kuei-jin (awakening the P'o through depravity or traumatic death, requiring Willpower 5+); the journey through the Yomi World and the Second Breath (reanimation); burial rites and grave customs; the chih-mei (feral, newly-risen vampires) and the hin training/testing period (the Fire and Water Test); the wu (5-person vampire "family," the "Hundred Corpse Families") and the direction system (north/west/east/south/center, each with a social role); kôa (a mortal-lifetime-long vampiric adolescence). Details the Yama Kings (evil spirit-lords of Yomi who corrupt Kuei-jin into akuma "devils") and the social hierarchy/rank system (chih-mei → hin → disciple → jina → mandarin → ancestor → bodhisattva → arhat), including the Three Dialogues test for ancestorship. Covers shadow war (ritualized inter-Kuei-jin conflict, in "twilight" and "midnight" forms) and Facing the Eye of Heaven (execution/suicide by sunlight). Describes akuma and heimin (outcast intermediaries with Kindred). Ends with the Great Principle/Fivefold Way (the five Ways: Origin, Lineage, Integrity, Obligation, Propriety) and an extended creation myth: the Two (Ebon Dragon/Yin and Scarlet Queen/Yang), the Great Cycle of Being, and the fall of the Wan Xian ("Ten Thousand Immortals") into the Wan Kuei ("Ten Thousand Demons") across the First through Fifth Ages.
**Key themes:** Social hierarchy and earned status; karmic responsibility; myth of a fallen golden age; ritualized conflict as an alternative to Kindred-style anarchy.
**Key terms/entities:** Dragon nests/tracks (p.22), chih-mei (p.26), hin/ré training (p.26-27), wu/Hundred Corpse Families (p.28), direction (north/west/center/east/south, p.28), kôa (p.29), Yama Kings (p.30) — named examples Mikaboshi, Emma-o, Tou Mu, Rangda; shadow war (twilight/midnight, p.31); Facing the Eye of Heaven (p.31); social ranks: chih-mei, hin, disciple, jina, mandarin, ancestor (+Mandate of Hell, Three Dialogues), bodhisattva, arhat (pp.32-35); akuma (p.36); heimin (p.36); the Great Principle/Fivefold Way — Origin, Lineage, Integrity, Obligation, Propriety (pp.38-41); origin myth — the Two/Ebon Dragon/Scarlet Queen, Great Cycle of Being, Wan Xian's fall (pp.42-45); Xue the Grand Arhat and his disciples Zao-lat (Saulot) and Ki (p.40).
**Notable claims/data points:** ~1 Kuei-jin per 100,000 mortals; China alone ~12,000 Kuei-jin; the five original Courts (Black Tortoise, White Tiger, Yellow Emperor, Azure Dragon, Scarlet Phoenix) and their historical collapse, with the Yellow Emperor Court's remnant becoming the Quincunx (cross-references Ch.6).

## Chapter Two: The Second Breath (pp. 46-73)
**Summary:** The Dharma (spiritual path) chapter. Explains the Dharmic journey as a sequence of stages (Sataa/death & rebirth, K'naa/the Hunger, Ré/instruction, Kôa/the Running Monkey, Dên/the Ten Dawns, Na Tou/the Wandering, Suru'a/finding a place, Dâh/All-Awareness) punctuated by satori-like flashes of insight (dên) and setbacks (diao). Gives full game systems for Dharma rolls (auspicious occasions vs. acts of blindness, each a resisted Virtue-vs-P'o roll) and a reference chart of Dharma levels 0-10 with associated benefits (Social bonus, Virtue/Trait maximums, Chi points, status, age). Surveys real-world Eastern belief systems as roleplaying flavor (Animism, Atheism, Buddhism, Confucianism, Hinduism, Islam, Shintoism, Taoism) and lists training/meditation methods (asceticism, benevolence, chants/mantras, cruelty, herbalism, incense, journeying, koans, meditation, mortification, violence, yantras/mandalas). Presents the five Dharmas in full: **Howl of the Devil-Tiger** (Virtue: P'o; embracing pain and demonhood as purification), **Way of the Resplendent Crane** (Virtue: Hun; law, correction, and purging corruption to avert the Sixth Age), **Song of the Shadow** (Virtue: Yin; bridging life and death, art and scholarship, "Bone Flowers"), **Path of a Thousand Whispers** (Virtue: Balance; living many sequential lives under different identities, the rarest Dharma), and **Dance of the Thrashing Dragon** (Virtue: Yang; animistic, hedonistic, life-force-hungry "Laughing Rainbows"). Each Dharma entry includes its founding myth (all trace to the arhat Xue), training style, weakness, omens/symbols, elemental/directional affiliation, concepts, eight tenets, and rival-path quotes.
**Key themes:** Enlightenment as a game-mechanical and narrative arc; the five Dharmas as opposed philosophies of monstrosity; Eastern religion as flavor rather than literal cosmology.
**Key terms/entities:** Satori/dên/diao (pp.50-51); kôa/Running Monkey (p.50); Dharma rating 0-10 chart with Social Bonus/Virtue Pair Max/Trait Max/Chi Points/Status/Age (p.54); the five Dharmas and their Virtues — Devil-Tiger (P'o, p.64), Resplendent Crane (Hun, p.66), Song of the Shadow (Yin, p.68), Thousand Whispers (Balance, p.70), Thrashing Dragon (Yang, p.72); Xue, Grand Arhat and founder-figure (p.48, recurring); sacred texts referenced — The Blood Sutras, The Songs of Hell, The Hollow Reed, The Midnight Jewel, The Empty Gate.
**Notable claims/data points:** Dharma changing/failure rules (four failed rolls = may switch paths; falling from Dharma 5+ bars return to that path; falling from all five = Final Death/oblivion).

## Chapter Three: The Ten Thousand Things (pp. 74-97)
**Summary:** Character creation chapter, built on the *Vampire: The Masquerade* framework but adapted for Kuei-jin specifics. Walks through the Second Breath and chih-mei period preceding play, the ré instruction period, and wu/guanxi (social-obligation network) formation. Covers Chi Balance (Yin-aspected, Yang-aspected, or Balanced characters) and how it shapes personality; the direction system's role in character concept; dual Natures (a standard Hun Nature/Archetype plus a P'o Nature/Archetype — Bandit, Barbarian, Deceiver, Demon, Fool, Legalist, Monkey, Slave); Attribute/Ability selection; an expanded Backgrounds list (including Jade Talisman and artifact-rated Backgrounds); Chi Virtues (Yin/Yang, Hun/P'o) in place of Kindred Virtues; Freebie Point costs; and Merits/Flaws specific to Kuei-jin (e.g., Akuma, Defiled, Different Body, Illiterate, Superstitious, Vengeful Ancestors).
**Key themes:** Mechanical translation of Kuei-jin cosmology into playable character options; dual-soul characterization (Hun Nature + P'o Nature) as a structural replacement for Kindred's single Nature/Demeanor.
**Key terms/entities:** Chi Balance — Yin/Yang/Balanced (p.80); P'o Archetypes — Bandit, Barbarian, Deceiver, Demon, Fool, Legalist, Monkey, Slave (p.92-93); Backgrounds incl. Jade Talisman, artifact ratings (p.86-87); Freebie Point cost chart (p.79).

## Chapter Four: Forces in the Land (pp. 98-131)
**Summary:** The Discipline chapter. Explains that Kuei-jin powers are grouped into Chi Arts (Equilibrium, Tapestry, Yin Prana, Yang Prana — direct Chi manipulation), Soul Arts (Cultivation, Obligation — mastery of Hun/P'o), Demon Arts (Black Wind, Hellweaving, Iron Mountain, Kiai — P'o-derived powers), and the five **Shintai** ("god body") transformation Disciplines tied to an elemental/Chi system: Blood Shintai (Water), Bone Shintai (Metal, incl. the Ch'ing Shih sub-state), Jade Shintai (Earth), Flesh Shintai (Wood), and Ghost-Flame Shintai (Fire, incl. "goblin" powers and the hima/goblin lantern form). Explicitly notes Kuei-jin and Kindred Disciplines are mutually exclusive and rooted in different sources (Chi/soul mastery vs. Caine's Blood). Chapter closes with Chi Rites (ritual magic, e.g. Trace the Dragon's Blood, Cloak the Dragon's Passage, Creating the Rivergate).
**Key themes:** Chi and the dual soul as the mechanical and thematic root of all Kuei-jin supernatural power, in explicit contrast to Kindred's Blood-based Disciplines.
**Key terms/entities:** Chi Arts (Equilibrium, Tapestry, Yin Prana, Yang Prana); Soul Arts (Cultivation, Obligation); Demon Arts (Black Wind, Hellweaving, Iron Mountain, Kiai); Shintai Disciplines — Blood, Bone (Ch'ing Shih), Jade, Flesh, Ghost-Flame (goblin powers); Chi Rites.

## Chapter Five: Arts of the Kuei-jin (pp. 132-151)
**Summary:** Further systems chapter covering Kuei-jin-specific mechanics: aggravated damage sources, the impossibility of Blood Bonding Kuei-jin vitae, permanent vs. temporary Yin/Yang, feeding and the Hunger, the several methods of obtaining Chi (flesh, blood, breath, essence), Chi imbalance and its correction, martial arts styles (soft/hard), Faith's effect on Kuei-jin, Final Death and Torpor (the "Little Death"), fire and sunlight rules (rot rather than burn), a table of typical ambient-Chi levels by location type (relevant to dragon nests), and spirit-travel systems (e.g., "Following in the Crab's Footsteps," crossing time via successes).
**Key themes:** Mechanical codification of the Chi economy and the "Little Death"/torpor as a distinctly Kuei-jin form of vulnerability.
**Key terms/entities:** Chi imbalance and correction (p.139-140); Little Death/torpor (p.144); ambient Chi table by location type (p.143); spirit-travel systems (p.145-146).

## Chapter Six: The Middle Kingdom (pp. 152-185)
**Summary:** The setting chapter, in three parts: history, the modern Middle Kingdom, and the Yin/Yang/Yomi Worlds. History covers China (the Yuan Dynasty, the "Closing and Humiliation" — Opium Wars-era Western incursion, the Communist takeover), Japan (the Serpent-Borne vampires' arrival, the Shogunate periods, the coming of the West, the "Divine Wind"/WWII, Hiroshima and Nagasaki as spiritual wounds, postwar "Return of the Foreigners"), and Korea (the Green Courts). The modern section gives Beijing in detail (the Forbidden City, the Mao Zedong Mausoleum as a spirit-haunted site, the "Underground City" beneath Qianmen, the hutongs, feng shui and the black-market "Silent Mandarins"), Hong Kong (Central District, Causeway Bay, the Squatters, Hollywood Road, Kowloon, "Joss," Uptown), Seoul, Bangkok, and Southeast Asia generally (Chinatowns, Cambodia and Laos, the "Great Leap Outward" of Golden Court expansion). The final section details the Yin World (incl. the Dark Kingdom of Jade and the Whispering Jungle), the Yang World (incl. the Realm of Fighting Spirits and Umi, the Dragon Kingdom of the Sea), the Yomi World/Thousand Hells (incl. named Hell-realms: the Wicked City, ruled by Yama King Mikaboshi; Kakuri, ruled by Emma-o and served exclusively by female shikome vampires; the Hell of Being Skinned Alive, ruled by Yama Queen Tou Mu), and the largely inaccessible Heavenly Realms.
**Key themes:** Asia as an "otherworldly" setting even to Western vampires; historical trauma (colonialism, WWII) as spiritual wounding; the Yomi World as the physical/cosmological source of Kuei-jin's demonic nature.
**Key terms/entities:** China/Japan/Korea history (pp.155-181); Beijing — Forbidden City, Mao Mausoleum, Underground City/Qianmen, hutongs, Silent Mandarins (pp.155-166); Hong Kong — Central, Causeway Bay, Squatters, Hollywood Road, Kowloon, Joss (pp.166-179); Yin World — Dark Kingdom of Jade, Whispering Jungle (p.183); Yang World — Realm of Fighting Spirits, Umi (p.183-184); Yomi World/Thousand Hells — Wicked City/Mikaboshi, Kakuri/Emma-o/shikome, Hell of Being Skinned Alive/Tou Mu (p.184-185); Heavenly Realms (p.185).
**Notable claims/data points:** The Great Cycle/Wheel of Ages framing is explicitly tied to real historical events (Hiroshima/Nagasaki described as the "Shadow Dragon" breathing plague-fire, WWII as "the Thousand Scarlet Devils" renaming the Age "Year Zero" — see Introduction p.8).

## Chapter Seven: Storytelling (pp. 186-197)
**Summary:** Guidance for running Kindred of the East chronicles. Frames core themes as Balance vs. Imbalance, Tradition vs. New Ways, and Group vs. Individual, plus a Morality section on Kuei-jin ethics without Western sin/redemption framing. Moods discussed: Exoticism (the East as unfamiliar even to Kindred) and Violence. Surveys conflict types the Storyteller can build stories around: Kuei-jin vs. Kuei-jin, Wu vs. Wu, Kuei-jin vs. Kin-jin (Western vampires), vs. Hengeyokai, vs. Spirits, vs. Mortals — and contrasts running a chronicle set in the Middle Kingdom versus one following Kuei-jin operating in the West.
**Key themes:** Balance/imbalance as narrative engine; exoticism as a deliberate mood; East-vs-West as a source of story conflict.
**Key terms/entities:** Themes — Balance vs. Imbalance, Tradition vs. New Ways, Group vs. Individual, Morality (pp.188-197); Moods — Exoticism, Violence; conflict types (Kuei-jin vs. Kuei-jin/Wu/Kin-jin/Hengeyokai/Spirits/Mortals).

## Chapter Eight: Rivals and Barbarians (pp. 198-222)
**Summary:** A bestiary/rivals chapter covering the other shen (supernatural beings) of the Middle Kingdom. Covers the hengeyokai ("those who change," shapeshifters) and their tribes; Awakened mages, split between the Technocracy and the Akashic Brotherhood and other Chi'n ta sects, with sample Chi'n ta character archetypes (Young Martial Artist, Metal Dragon Enforcer, Grand Chinese Wizard); the Kith (Asian fae), including the Tanuki Trickster; the Ten Thousand Spirits with full game systems (Willpower, Chi Reservoir, Tasks, Charms) and sample spirits (e.g. Chih-Chiang-Fyu-ya, the demon archer); the Dead of the Jade Kingdom (Asian wraiths) — Powers and Potentates, Citizens of the Empire, Bureaucrats of the Jade Palace, Beasts of Yin; other Middle Kingdom denizens (bakemono, dhampyrs, chih-mei recap); and a sample artifact list (Level One through Four artifacts, e.g. the Wayang Sash).
**Key themes:** The Middle Kingdom as a crowded supernatural ecology in which Kuei-jin are one power among many, not unchallenged rulers.
**Key terms/entities:** Hengeyokai/"the Tribes"; Technocracy and Akashic Brotherhood/Chi'n ta; Kith/Tanuki Trickster; Ten Thousand Spirits (Chih-Chiang-Fyu-ya); Dead of the Jade Kingdom (Jade Palace bureaucracy, Beasts of Yin); bakemono; dhampyrs; artifact tiers and the Wayang Sash.

## Cross-cutting themes
- **Balance vs. imbalance**: the book's stated central theme (Introduction, Ch.7), expressed cosmologically (Yin/Yang), socially (courts, directions), and personally (Hun/P'o, Chi Balance) — recurs in Ch.1, 2, 3, 5, 7.
- **The Wheel of Ages / coming apocalypse**: the Fifth Age's decay and the looming Sixth Age (Kuei-jin's Gehenna-equivalent) frame nearly every chapter, from the Introduction's cosmology through Ch.6's real-world history (Hiroshima/Nagasaki as omens) to Ch.7's storytelling guidance.
- **Earned hierarchy vs. Kindred's Blood-based generation**: Kuei-jin status comes from Dharma advancement and social testing (hin training, Three Dialogues) rather than lineage — contrasted explicitly with Kindred throughout Ch.1 and the Introduction's Kuei-jin/Kindred comparison list.
- **The Yomi World**: introduced in Ch.1 (the soul's journey before rebirth) and detailed geographically in Ch.6 (Wicked City, Kakuri, Hell of Being Skinned Alive) — this is the same setting *The 1000 Hells* (a later sourcebook in this library) expands at book length.
- **Xue and the five Dharmas**: founding myths for all five Dharmas trace to the arhat Xue (Ch.2); the Ki Chuan (Xue's disciple Ki's commentaries) is referenced as the central Kuei-jin scripture across Ch.1 and Ch.2.
