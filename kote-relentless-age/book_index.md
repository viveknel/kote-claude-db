# Kindred of the East: The Relentless Age — Index

## How to use this file
This is a thematic index of *Kindred of the East: The Relentless Age*, a fan-made "spiritual reboot" of the *Kindred of the East* line, built on *Vampire: the Masquerade 20th Anniversary Edition* (V20) rules. **Read the warning below before using this alongside the other books in this library** — it is not a sequel, prequel, or expansion of them; it renames almost everything. For exact quotes, precise rules numbers, or specific named NPCs/artifacts, query `book_chunks.db` (see `README.md` in this bundle) rather than relying on the summaries below.

## Important: a different, non-canonical edition — read this first
This book is **not part of the same continuity** as this library's other five books (`kote-core`, `kote-companion`, `kote-shadow-war`, `kote-1000-hells`, `kote-blood-silk`). It is a fan work by a single author ("hsienfan," developed with an RPGnet community thread — see Credits, p.4) self-published through Storytellers Vault, explicitly framed as celebrating "25 Years of Kindred of the East" and built to run on **V20** (*Vampire: the Masquerade 20th Anniversary Edition*) rather than the original mid-1990s *Vampire* rules the other five books assume. Note on dating: the indicia block reads "© 2017 White Wolf Entertainment AB" (p.4), but this is very likely reused boilerplate — the 25th-anniversary framing (1998 + 25 = 2023) and the PDF's own creation metadata (December 2024) both point to a 2023-2024-era work, not a 2017 one; treat the printed 2017 date with suspicion rather than as fact. **Authorship note:** this same "hsienfan" also wrote *In the Realm of Gods and Dreams*, a fan-made Changeling: The Dreaming supplement (indexed separately, in a different library) credited to "Sebastian Noh (Hsienfan)" with an identical "developed... with assistance from RPGnet" credit line and the same named collaborators (Coco Vanille, Tommy Lee) — see this bundle's README for the full comparison.

**The overlap with the other five books is thematic, not factual.** Both continuities are set in "1998" and share some vocabulary (Chi, Dharma, Yama Kings, Thousand Hells, Suffering, the number "Mikaboshi" as a Wicked-City-ruling Yama King, "Emma"/"Enma" as a hell-lord name) and structural DNA (a five/nine-Dharma spiritual-path system, a Quincunx-named political entity, a coming apocalyptic "Age of Sorrows"/Sixth Age). But almost every proper noun, the entire soul/Virtue system, the character-creation math, and the setting's political history are different and **contradict** the other books if read as one continuity:

- **Species name**: "Hungry Dead," never "Kuei-jin," "Wan Kuei," or "Cathayan."
- **Soul system**: four Virtues (Renunciation/Righteousness/Vitality/Wickedness) replace the other books' two-soul Hun/P'o structure entirely — there is no Hun, no P'o, and no Yin/Yang Chi-aspect system here.
- **Dharmas**: entirely renamed and reorganized into three tiers — Petals of Virtue (Bone Flowers, Devil-Tigers, Resplendent Cranes, Thrashing Dragons), the Mirror and the Wheel (Diamond Serpents, Rootless Trees), and the Independents (Celestial Tribe, Ram-Hearted, Mayaparisatya) — plus Outsiders (Cerulean Veils, Scorpion Eaters). None of the other library books' five Dharma names (Devil-Tiger/P'o, Resplendent Crane/Hun, Song of the Shadow/Yin, Thousand Whispers/Balance, Thrashing Dragon/Yang) map cleanly onto these; "Devil-Tiger," "Resplendent Crane," and "Thrashing Dragon" are reused as names but attached to a different Virtue/soul system and different tenets.
- **The Quincunx**: in this book, a literal empire that ruled China 1449-1979 (the "Five August Courts" of Chang'an, Chongqing, Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou) before being violently overthrown by the anti-dharmic "Scorpion Eaters" in the 1979 "Century Rebellion" (Ch.1, p.16; Ch.6, p.130-134). This flatly contradicts `kote-companion`'s 1304 CE Treaty of the Quincunx and its five *different* Courts (Blood/Flesh/Flame/Bone/Jade, still functioning in the modern nights across the rest of this library) — the two Quincunxes share a name and a five-Courts structure and nothing else.
- **Character creation**: built on V20 math (Attributes 7/5/3, Abilities 13/9/5, Backgrounds 5 dots) rather than the original *Vampire* rules the rest of the library's mechanics assume.

**Practical guidance for a future session:** if a query pulls a chunk from this book (`kote-relentless-age`) alongside chunks from the other five books, do not silently merge them into one answer — name which continuity each fact comes from. Cross-book search results that mix this book with the others will produce apparent "contradictions" that are really just two unrelated re-imaginings of the same source material. See the top-level `README.md` and `library_index.md` for how this is flagged at the library level.

## Overview
*Kindred of the East: The Relentless Age* is a complete, self-contained reimagining of the 1998 *Kindred of the East* line for V20. It retells the same basic premise — undead "Hungry Dead" who escaped the Thousand Hells and now pursue enlightenment (Dharma) across a hidden Asian night world — but rebuilds the cosmology, Dharmas, powers (Demon Arts/Soul Arts/Godbody Arts instead of Chi Arts/Soul Arts/Shintai), and setting politics from scratch, explicitly bidding for inclusivity and modern sensibility (recurring "is this a bad idea to play if I'm not Asian/Chinese/etc." sidebars) and offering extensive optional systems (Dharmic sects, Suffering-eruption generators, "Icons of Fate") a Storyteller can bolt on. The book runs Introduction through Chapter Six (character concept/rules, powers, Storytelling advice, and an eleven-region gazetteer), then two appendices (spirits/other-supernaturals rules plus sample characters; a full mortal Demon Hunter subsystem) and an errata/clarifications chapter.

**Page-numbering note:** the source PDF is a 2-page-spread export (100 PDF pages, each containing two printed book pages side by side). This bundle's `book_chunks.db` stores the **lower/even printed page number of each spread** as `page` — e.g. `page=12` contains the text of both printed page 12 *and* printed page 13. To look up an odd printed page, query the even page just below it (printed p.13 → `--page 12`). See `README.md` for the full conversion note.

## Introduction (pp. 4-12)
**Summary:** Opens with a framing-fiction vignette (a newly-risen Hungry One recruited in a Hakata, Japan restaurant) and a "Myths and Realities" sidebar establishing baseline facts (Hungry Dead are not ghosts, are "technically" vampires but often eat flesh, and are vulnerable to sunlight/fire/holy weapons). Lays out the book's chapter structure, then gives an extensive A-Z lexicon of setting terminology (pp.8-12) covering realms, ranks, key Dharma-adjacent terms, and named entities referenced throughout the rest of the book.
**Key themes:** Framing the Hungry Dead condition through myth-vs-reality contrast; the "Relentless Age" itself — defined as the 108 years between a Hungry One's rise and their serious pursuit of enlightenment (p.6).
**Key terms/entities:** Hungry Dead/Second Breath (p.6); the Relentless Age (108-year span, p.6); Age of Sorrows / Demon Emperor prophecy (p.6); Quincunx (empire, 1449-1979, p.8); Wheel of Ages / twelve-age cycle (p.8-12 lexicon); Great Wheel and its five realms (p.8-9 lexicon); Petals of Virtue / Demon Immortals (p.8); Scorpion Eater Societies (p.8); Yama King, generic definition (p.12).
**Notable claims/data points:** Sets the story in 1998, same nominal year as `kote-core`'s modern-day setting, but an unrelated continuity (see warning above). The lexicon explicitly states "Chi" and "Dharma" are "authorial terms of convenience" (echoed again on p.18) — a deliberate acknowledgment that the setting borrows vocabulary without claiming to be a direct translation of any single real-world language.

## Chapter One: Welcome to the Night World (pp. 14-30)
**Summary:** The society/cosmology chapter. Opens with a real-world-grounded snapshot of "the World of Darkness in 1998" across Tokyo, Mongolia, India, and the Asian Financial Crisis, then a two-part "Asians Only?" sidebar addressing whether the game is only for Asian players/settings (answer: no, with pointers to non-Asian material in Ch.6 and Appendix I). Covers the core Hungry Dead condition ("The Hungry Dead: A Primer" — Back from the Dead, Suffering, Hunger), a lexicon-adjacent "Local Terminology" sidebar (translations of "Chi" across nine Asian languages) and the universal Dharma-rank ladder (Larva → Celebrant → Aspirant → Dedicant → Master → Grandmaster → Great Sage → All-Awakened/Conquerors). Details the five realms of the Great Wheel (Mortal World, Underworld — ruled by Qin Shi Huang as "Yu Huang" — Spirit Wilds, Heavens, Thousand Hells) and, in "Contenders for the Throne" (p.24-25), profiles eight named Hell-realms whose Yama Kings are vying to become the prophesied Demon Emperor, including the Wicked City (ruled by a figure called "Mikaboshi"). Closes with "Fellows of the Night" — the Beast Courts, Little Gods, Half-Devils, Dhampyrs (+ "Creating a Dhampyr" sidebar), mortal wizards/witches, Demon Hunter families (regional examples: Japan's fallen Yanagi clan and corporate Okamoto/Strike Force Zero; Rajasthan Sikhs; southern Chinese wandering warriors; South Korea's "Warden Kim"), and the history of Hungry Dead/Cainite ("Draculas") contact.
**Key themes:** Real-world grounding in 1998 Asia; the coming Age of Sorrows as a multipolar succession crisis among Yama Kings rather than a single inevitable apocalypse; explicit inclusivity framing for non-Asian players.
**Key terms/entities:** Second Breath, Suffering, Hunger (p.16); Dharma ranks Larva through All-Awakened/Conquerors (p.18); the five realms — Mortal World, Underworld/Yu Huang, Spirit Wilds, Heavens/Lower Heavens, Thousand Hells (p.20-23); named contending Hell-realms — Hell of Being Skinned Alive/Iron Queen, Hell of Boiling Oil/the Unnameable, Hell of Burrowing Maggots/Mistress of Pestilence, Hell of the Burning Seas/Mother of the Depths, Hell of Upside-Down Sinners, Lanka the Demon City/King Ravana, the Night Kingdom/Lord Enma, the Pit of Salt and Iron, the Wicked City/"Mikaboshi" (p.24-25); Beast Courts, Little Gods, Half-Devils, Dhampyrs (p.26-28); Demon Hunter families and named regional groups (p.28-29); Book of Nod referenced as (mis)interpreted Hungry Dead scripture by Western Cainites (p.28).
**Notable claims/data points:** Hungry Dead only learned Cainites were a separate species "about three centuries ago" (p.28-29) — a late-contact history distinct from `kote-companion`'s more extensive Kin-jin material.

## Chapter Two: The Enlightenment of Monsters (pp. 32-56)
**Summary:** The Dharma chapter. Introduces the four Virtues (Renunciation/Ghost Soul, Righteousness/Heavenly Soul, Vitality/Beast Soul, Wickedness/Devil Soul) as the four "partial souls" every Hungry Dead is fractured into, then presents the full Dharma roster in three groupings. **The Petals of Virtue** ("Immortal Dharmas," Chinese-origin): Bone Flowers (death/ghosts, Ghost Arts), Devil-Tigers (embracing Wickedness as purification, Devil Arts), Resplendent Cranes (harmony/order, Heavenly Arts), Thrashing Dragons (life-affirming hedonism, prolific Dhampyr-siring). **The Mirror and the Wheel** ("Human Dharmas," South Asian-origin): Diamond Serpents (retained humanity/family ties) and Rootless Trees (serial human identities, no fixed self). **The Independents** (South Asian): Celestial Tribe (self-styled godlings, Devotion Godbody), Ram-Hearted (Islamic "Eastern Road of Heaven," community defense — plus a dormant "Western Road of Heaven" variant), Mayaparisatya (Pure Illusion-Truth, nihilist/liberationist, ties to Clan Ravnos). **The Outsiders**: Cerulean Veils (animist spirit-tenders, Mongolia-rooted, with a heretical "Kin of Izanami" Japanese variant) and Scorpion Eaters (anti-Dharmic rejectionists, capped at Dharma 1). Each Dharma entry gives death/rebirth flavor, three player-concept hooks, tenets, and Suffering triggers.
**Key themes:** Enlightenment as multipolar and contested rather than a single true path; the Petals/Mirror-and-Wheel/Independents/Outsiders grouping as a geography-of-belief structure (China vs. South Asia vs. rejectionist).
**Key terms/entities:** the four Virtues (p.32-33); Bone Flowers, Devil-Tigers, Resplendent Cranes, Thrashing Dragons (p.34-39); Diamond Serpents, Rootless Trees, and the Great Clans variant Dharma (Japan) (p.40-45); Celestial Tribe (Descended vs. Faces of the Goddess split), Ram-Hearted, Mayaparisatya (p.46-51); Western Road of Heaven variant (p.50); Cerulean Veils, Kin of Izanami variant, Scorpion Eaters (p.52-56).
**Notable claims/data points:** Two more Dharmas are flagged as described elsewhere rather than here — the heretical Ebon Masks (Ch.6, China) and the Sikhi "Dharma of the Lion" (Appendix I) — plus a Dharmic-sects subsystem for splinter groups (Ch.5) (p.56).

## Chapter Three: Character Creation and Systems (pp. 58-76)
**Summary:** Full V20-based character creation: Concept/Nature/Demeanor/Dharma/Destiny questions, then standard V20 Attribute/Ability dot spreads, Virtue assignment (baseline 1 dot each, 7 more to assign), Demon Art/Regalia selection, and a freebie-point cost table. Introduces three new Backgrounds — Talisman, Dragon's Nest, and Rituals (with worked example items/rites) — and a Dharma-linked trait-maximum/Chi-pool/Godbody-cap progression table (Dharma 1-10). Covers core systems: Suffering (gain/loss triggers, eruption severity tiers by accumulated points, an optional random-eruption generator table cross-referenced to Ch.5), Chi (feeding methods gated by Dharma rank, including "breath" and "world" feeding at higher ranks), the Lesser Death (torpor-equivalent), Virtue cultivation and Soul Imbalance (with a new Merit, Soul Virtuoso), Dharma gain/loss (including deliberately sacrificing a Dharma dot to wipe Suffering), rules for Hungry Dead who end up following Cainite Paths of Enlightenment or raw Humanity instead of Dharma, and the endgame "Rite of the Hundred Clouds" ascension system (with a full worked example, Mari of the Blue Dawn).
**Key themes:** Enlightenment as a trackable, sacrificeable resource in constant tension with Suffering; character creation as a direct V20 adaptation rather than a from-scratch system.
**Key terms/entities:** Talisman, Dragon's Nest, Rituals Backgrounds (p.62-67); Gauntlet rating chart (p.64); major rituals — Bond of the Night Family, Contain Suffering, Enlightenment's Artery, the Forked Road, Infant Devil Godmother, Obeisance to the Great Masters, Peace of Monsters, Punishment of Heaven's Eye, Spirit Banquet (p.64-67); Suffering system and eruption tiers (p.69-70); Chi system and feeding methods (p.70); Soul Imbalance (p.71-72); Dharma gain/loss, Cainite Paths, Humanity-track Hungry Dead (p.72-74); Rite of the Hundred Clouds (p.74-76).
**Notable claims/data points:** Scorpion Eaters are hard-capped at Dharma 1 by character-creation rule (p.58-59, p.74), mechanically enforcing their anti-enlightenment philosophy from Ch.2.

## Chapter Four: Arts (pp. 78-118)
**Summary:** The powers chapter, in three tiers. **Demon Arts** (innate, Suffering-fueled, uncapped) — Black Wind, Devil Fist, Iron Mountain, and Demon Regalia, the latter with a full catalog of hell-themed mutations/weapons organized by the Hell a character's power resembles (Being Skinned Alive, Boiling Oil, Burning Seas, Upside-Down Sinners, Lanka, the Night Kingdom, Pit of Salt and Iron, the Wicked City), plus rules for playing Half-Devils. **Soul Arts** (Chi-fueled, gated by Virtue rank) — full technique lists for all four Virtues (Renunciation/Ghost Arts, Righteousness/Heavenly Arts, Vitality/Beast Arts, Wickedness/Devil Arts), the master-level "Dragon Tear" that draws on all four Virtues at once (with an "Eating Souls" sidebar on consuming a captured soul), and the forbidden "Hellweaving" art that runs on Suffering instead of Virtue/Chi (with a parallel "Selling Your Soul" sidebar on pact-based power). **Godbodies** (Dharma-gated master transformations) — nine full Godbody writeups: Ash and Flame, Blood, Bone, Devotion, Flesh, Illusion, Jade, Poxes, and Storms, each with four preparations plus a mastery-level Godbody effect. Closes with "Combining Arts" — worked example Conjunctive Arts that splice a Soul Art technique with a Godbody preparation.
**Key themes:** A three-tier power structure (innate/corrupting Demon Arts vs. cultivated Soul Arts vs. Dharma-locked Godbodies) that mirrors the book's Suffering/Chi/Dharma resource triangle from Ch.3.
**Key terms/entities:** Black Wind, Devil Fist, Iron Mountain, Demon Regalia by Hell-of-origin (p.78-81); Ghost Arts, Heavenly Arts, Beast Arts, Devil Arts full technique lists (p.82-93); the Dragon Tear (p.94-98); Hellweaving (p.96-99); the nine Godbodies — Ash/Flame, Blood, Bone, Devotion, Flesh, Illusion, Jade, Poxes, Storms (p.100-115); Conjunctive Arts (p.116-117).
**Notable claims/data points:** A design-note sidebar (p.117) explains Dharma is deliberately never rolled directly alongside Willpower in these systems — Dharma is framed as "a spiritual key," not a raw power stat, a stated design philosophy for anyone building further homebrew Arts.

## Chapter Five: Storytelling in the Relentless Age (pp. 120-128)
**Summary:** Storytelling guidance split into two halves. "Playing with Theme and Mood" walks through the book's five core themes — Hell, Hunger, Enlightenment, and Fate and Destiny — each with discussion questions and an optional mechanical tool (the Golden Affliction, a Little-God-blood addiction track; Dharmic Sects, letting groups reinterpret a tenet for bonus Suffering-cleansing; a random Suffering-eruption generator table; Icons of Fate, a destiny-signs minigame). "Playing with Different Cultures: Some Advice to Start" is an extended real-world sensitivity-and-research essay — cautioning against playing marginalized real-world identities (e.g. Rohingya, buraku, Zainichi Korean, Dalit characters) without deep research, urging "session zero" and X-card practices, and closing with encouragement to use modern research tools rather than be paralyzed by unfamiliarity.
**Key themes:** Explicit meta-level guidance on responsible cross-cultural roleplay, presented as being just as core to running the game as the mechanical Theme/Mood material.
**Key terms/entities:** Hell, Hunger, Enlightenment, Fate and Destiny (p.120-125); the Golden Affliction (p.121); Dharmic Sects, with five worked examples (p.124-125); Suffering Eruption Generator table (p.122-123); Icons of Fate (p.126); session zero / X-cards (p.127).
**Notable claims/data points:** The chapter omits "Hunger" from its own four-theme header text after having described five themes in the Introduction's table of contents summary (p.6-7 lists Hell, Hunger, Enlightenment, Fate, Destiny) — Fate and Destiny are treated as one combined theme in practice (p.125), a minor internal inconsistency worth knowing about if cross-referencing the ToC blurb against the actual chapter.

## Appendix I: Special Systems and Example Characters (pp. 168-188)

**Key terms/entities:** Spirit-building system, Rank/Aspects/Thematic Actions/Taboos (p.168-169); seven example spirits (p.169-173); other-supernatural quick-build rules and five examples (p.174-177); thirteen sample characters (p.178-188).
**Notable claims/data points:** An "Inevitable Comparison" sidebar (p.183) directly addresses Hungry-Dead-vs-Cainite combat balance, conceding Hungry Dead are individually stronger in a vacuum but arguing context (Suffering costs, the wider supernatural ecology) keeps this from being a one-sided matchup — useful if a chronicle ever needs to referee a fight between this book's Hungry Dead and mainline V20 Cainites.

## Appendix II: The Way of the Demon Hunter (pp. 189-194)
**Summary:** A complete mortal Demon Hunter subsystem: an "Awakened Chi" resource (1 free bound/spent point, expandable at a lethal-damage cost) powering three Battle Arts trees tied to the mortal Virtues — Conscience (Deny Sustenance, Ensnare Evil, Resist Illusions), Self-Control (Mastery of Wounds/Might/Movement), and Courage (the Killing Art, the Purity of Fire, Spirit Sight) — plus a new Hunter's Talisman Background (five dot ratings, two worked examples: Electroshock Gloves and the Azure-Wave Sword). The appendix closes with an unlabeled bonus section, "Appendix ???: The Thirteenth Generation," a short essay-and-guidelines block for reskinning this entire book's systems to run a straight, non-Kindred-of-the-East *Vampire: the Masquerade* chronicle organized around generational apocalyptic prophecy instead of Dharma.
**Key themes:** Giving mortals a structurally parallel (Virtue-gated, Chi-adjacent) power system to the Hungry Dead's own, rather than leaving Demon Hunters mechanically inert.
**Key terms/entities:** Awakened Chi (p.189); Battle Arts of Conscience/Self-Control/Courage (p.189-191); Hunter's Talisman Background (p.191); "The Thirteenth Generation" conversion notes (p.192-193).
**Notable claims/data points:** The Thirteenth Generation section is explicitly framed as optional and non-canonical even *within* this already-noncanonical book — it's a toolkit for running a *different* game using these rules, not additional lore about the Hungry Dead setting itself.

## Errata and Clarifications (pp. 195-196)
**Summary:** A short post-publication corrections chapter, folding fixes directly into rules the rest of the book states elsewhere. Clarifies that accumulating Suffering to activate multiple Demon Arts at once is instantaneous with no concentration time; details Dhampyr "Luck-Binding" (an advanced power converting the standing Luck pool into semi-permanent Ability bonuses); confirms all Godbody preparations default to one-scene duration; clarifies Godbody of Flame fire-control mechanics; and clarifies that spirits summoned/awakened via certain Soul Arts (Dream-Being, Goblin Cat, Stone Lion) are temporary unless fed Chi continuously, becoming permanent after a full lunar month.
**Key themes:** None beyond direct rules maintenance — this section is reference material, not narrative content.
**Key terms/entities:** Demon Art activation timing (p.195); Dhampyr Luck-Binding (p.195); Godbody duration defaults (p.195); Godbody of Flame clarifications (p.195); Soul Art spirit-rooting permanence rule (p.196).

## Cross-cutting themes
- **The Age of Sorrows / Demon Emperor succession crisis** is this book's central plot engine, introduced in Ch.1's "Contenders for the Throne" (p.24-25) and given local expression in nearly every Ch.6 region — Korea's Wicked City corruption (p.144-145), China's Ebon Masks prophecy about a "perfect" cursed text (p.132), Laos's naga-bound Ebon Masks (p.155-157) — making "Mikaboshi" and the Wicked City the closest thing this book has to a single recurring antagonist thread.
- **Orthodoxy vs. rejection** recurs at every scale: the Scorpion Eaters reject Dharma outright (Ch.2, capped at Dharma 1 in Ch.3); the Ebon Masks invert orthodox Dharma into a demon-worshipping heresy (Ch.6, China and Laos); Singapore's Devil-Tiger enforcers are secretly being recruited into rebellion by the Pure Illusion-Truth (Ch.6); even the Dharmic Sects optional system (Ch.5) is framed as splinter reinterpretation rather than obedience.
- **Explicit, repeated real-world cultural-sensitivity framing** — the "Asians Only?" sidebar (Ch.1, p.14), the full "Playing with Different Cultures" essay (Ch.5, p.126-128), and the "Playing as Non-Asian Hungry Dead" sidebar (Ch.6, p.165, tied to the California setting) — is unusually dense for a single sourcebook and forms its own throughline distinct from the setting lore.
- **The Suffering/Chi/Dharma resource triangle** established in Ch.3 structurally organizes Ch.4's three power tiers (Demon Arts run on Suffering, Soul Arts on Chi gated by Virtue, Godbodies gated by Dharma) and resurfaces as Ch.5's optional Suffering-eruption generator and Dharmic Sects tools — the mechanics and the Storytelling advice are tightly integrated rather than separate layers.
- **Half-Devils and Dhampyrs as recurring "designed to be interesting NPCs/PCs" categories**, introduced in Ch.1 (p.26-28), given full player-facing rules in Ch.3-4, and then anchoring entire Ch.6 locations (Yuzhong's all-Dhampyr town, p.133-135; Tibet's half-devil redemption library, p.150) rather than being one-off flavor.
