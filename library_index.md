# Kindred of the East Library — Cross-Book Index

## Books in this collection
- `kote-core/` — *Kindred of the East* (White Wolf, 1998) — 229 pages. The core rulebook: Kuei-jin cosmology, Dharmas, character creation, Disciplines, the Middle Kingdom setting, Storytelling guidance, and rival supernaturals.
- `kote-companion/` — *Kindred of the East Companion* (White Wolf) — 127 pages. Expands court history/politics, adds dhampyrs/yulan-jin and four heretical Dharmas, expands Disciplines, surveys real-world Asian religions, and covers Kuei-jin/Kindred cross-contact.
- `kote-shadow-war/` — *Shadow War* (White Wolf, 1999) — 103 pages. A full ritual-combat system (twilight/midnight/underground war), Scarlet Screens, and a chapter-length account of an ongoing Kuei-jin invasion of California ("Crimson War").
- `kote-1000-hells/` — *The Thousand Hells* (White Wolf, 1999) — 121 pages. Deep-dives Yomi Wan/the Thousand Hells: its geography, named Yama Kings, Storytelling guidance, Yomi-specific systems, and a full appendix on akuma.
- `kote-blood-silk/` — *World of Darkness: Blood & Silk* (White Wolf, 2000) — 177 pages. A historical prequel supplement set in 1197 CE, at the close of the Fourth Age — explains the origins of institutions (the Quincunx's predecessor courts, the Shintai Disciplines' Godbody ancestors) that the other four books treat as already established.
- `wind-from-the-east/` — *Wind from the East* (White Wolf, 2000) — 98 pages. A **Vampire: The Dark Ages** sourcebook (not a *Kindred of the East* line title) covering the Mongol Empire (c.1166-1388) from the Cainite/Western side — the Anda (a Gangrel offshoot) and Wu Zao (a Salubri offshoot) bloodlines, and a detailed portrait of the Black Tortoise Wan Kuei Court as seen from outside the *Kindred of the East* line. See "Important: a book shared between two libraries" below.
- `kote-relentless-age/` — *Kindred of the East: The Relentless Age* (fan-published via Storytellers Vault, built for V20; ~2023-2024 despite a "2017" indicia — see its own bundle README) — 196 pages. A complete, non-canonical reimagining of the whole line for *Vampire: the Masquerade 20th Anniversary Edition* — renamed species ("Hungry Dead"), a four-Virtue soul system replacing Hun/P'o, a fully restructured Dharma roster, a different Quincunx, powers, and an eleven-region gazetteer. **Read "Important: kote-relentless-age is a different, non-canonical edition" below before treating anything in it as consistent with the other six books.**

The first five books belong to the same *Kindred of the East* product line (a *Vampire: The Masquerade* spin-off focused on the Kuei-jin, Asian vampires distinct from Western Kindred/Cainites) and share one continuous, tightly cross-referenced continuity. *Wind from the East* is a *Vampire: The Dark Ages* book included here because it explicitly cross-references and builds on this line (particularly *Blood & Silk*, sharing its 1197 CE anchor point) — see the "Important: a book shared between two libraries" note below for how it relates to a second, separate library this same bundle may belong to. *The Relentless Age* is **not** part of that continuity at all — it's a separate, fan-made reimagining of the whole line included here because it's still a *Kindred of the East*-titled work a reader of this library would reasonably expect to find, not because it agrees with the other six books. See "Important: kote-relentless-age is a different, non-canonical edition" below before drawing on it for any fact-level synthesis.

## Important: a book shared between two libraries
`wind-from-the-east/` is deliberately included in **both** this Kindred of the East library and a separate **Vampire: The Masquerade / Vampire: The Dark Ages library** that may be built and read alongside this one in a future session. Its own bundle (`wind-from-the-east/README.md`) carries this same note. If both libraries are present together in a conversation, treat this book as the connective tissue between them and expect it to be relevant to cross-library questions about either line — no need to search for or assume the existence of the other library's other books; just note that this one is shared.

## Important: kote-relentless-age is a different, non-canonical edition
`kote-relentless-age/` (*Kindred of the East: The Relentless Age*) is a complete fan-made reimagining of the *Kindred of the East* line, built for *Vampire: the Masquerade 20th Anniversary Edition* (V20) rather than the original *Vampire* rules the other six books assume. It shares this library's shelf because it shares the *Kindred of the East* name and broad premise (undead who escaped the Thousand Hells pursuing Dharma/enlightenment in a hidden Asian night world, nominally set in "1998"), and it echoes some vocabulary and even a few proper nouns — but it is **not** a sequel, prequel, or expansion of the other six books, and treating it as one will produce false contradictions. Concretely, relative to the rest of this library:

- **Species name**: "Hungry Dead," never "Kuei-jin," "Wan Kuei," or "Cathayan."
- **Soul system**: four Virtues (Renunciation/Righteousness/Vitality/Wickedness) entirely replace the Hun/P'o dual-soul structure and the Yin/Yang Chi-aspect system the other six books share.
- **Dharmas**: renamed and reorganized into Petals of Virtue (Bone Flowers, Devil-Tigers, Resplendent Cranes, Thrashing Dragons), the Mirror and the Wheel (Diamond Serpents, Rootless Trees), the Independents (Celestial Tribe, Ram-Hearted, Mayaparisatya), and Outsiders (Cerulean Veils, Scorpion Eaters). A few names are reused (Devil-Tiger, Resplendent Crane, Thrashing Dragon) but attached to a different soul system and different tenets — these are false friends, not the same Dharmas under a new coat of paint.
- **The Quincunx**: here, a literal empire that ruled China 1449-1979 before being overthrown in the 1979 "Century Rebellion." This directly contradicts `kote-companion`'s 1304 CE Treaty of the Quincunx and its five *different* Courts (Blood/Flesh/Flame/Bone/Jade), which are still standing in the modern nights across the rest of this library. Same name, unrelated institution.
- **A few genuine echoes exist** and are worth knowing about rather than either ignoring or over-reading: "Mikaboshi" names a Yama King ruling something called "the Wicked City" in both this book and `kote-core`/`kote-1000-hells`, and "Emma"/"Enma" appears as a hell-lord name in both continuities too. Treat these as parallel reuse of real-world mythological names by two separate authors, not as evidence the settings connect.

**Practical guidance:** if a cross-book search pulls a chunk from `kote-relentless-age` alongside chunks from the other six books, don't silently merge them into one answer — name which continuity each fact comes from. See `kote-relentless-age/book_index.md`'s own "Important" section for the full detail, and `kote-relentless-age/README.md` for a page-numbering quirk specific to that book's source PDF.

## How to use this file
This file synthesizes themes across the six same-continuity books above (`kote-core`, `kote-companion`, `kote-shadow-war`, `kote-1000-hells`, `kote-blood-silk`, `wind-from-the-east`). `kote-relentless-age` is deliberately **not** folded into the "Cross-cutting themes," "Points of agreement," or "Points of disagreement or tension" sections below, since those sections describe one shared continuity and it isn't part of it — see the "Important" note above instead. For details on any single book, read that book's own `book_index.md` in its subdirectory — each is a complete, standalone reference. For exact quotes or specific facts, query that book's `book_chunks.db` directly, or use `scripts/query_library.py` to search across all seven databases at once (see usage below).

## Cross-cutting themes

### 1. The Quincunx and court politics
- **kote-core** (Ch.1, Ch.6) introduces the concept of Kuei-jin "courts" and lists five historical Courts (Black Tortoise, White Tiger, Yellow Emperor, Azure Dragon, Scarlet Phoenix) that collapsed, with the Yellow Emperor Court's remnant becoming "the Quincunx" — but gives no founding date or mechanism.
- **kote-companion** (Ch.1) supplies the missing history: the 1304 CE **Treaty of the Quincunx**, signed at Shaolin by the surviving Black Tortoise/White Tiger/Yellow Emperor ancestors, which dissolved the old five Courts and created the five *new* ones actually used in modern play — Blood Court (Beijing), Flesh Court (Shanghai), Flame Court (Hong Kong), Bone Court (Chongqing), and Jade Court (Changan) — plus the rotating figurehead "Bestowed Ancestor of the Celestial Realms." It also explains why the Azure Dragon and Scarlet Phoenix courts are estranged: they refused to sign and were stripped of authority, becoming the modern Japanese Uji and Golden Courts respectively.
- **kote-blood-silk** (Ch.1, Appendix) supplies the *prequel*: writing from 1197 CE — over a century before the Treaty — it shows the Yellow Emperor Court still centered at Changan under ancestor Wang Hongben, with the Azure Dragon Court "slowly collapsing" in real time. The Appendix's Changan write-up is thus a direct, dated snapshot of the institution that becomes the Jade Court of the Quincunx.
- **kote-shadow-war** (Ch.5, "Crimson War") shows the Blood Court (as the Quincunx's seat) acting with real authority in the present day — Ancestor Ch'ang personally interrogating a Kindred researcher — demonstrating the Treaty's centralizing effect still holding centuries later.
- **wind-from-the-east** (Ch.2) supplies a fifth data point from outside the *Kindred of the East* line entirely: a full narrative account of the Black Tortoise Court specifically (rather than the Quincunx as a whole) across exactly the same span the other books only gesture at — its Fourth Age founding by three *wu*, its centuries-long feud with the Anda bloodline, its 1304 CE decision to join the Treaty of the Quincunx (confirming the Companion's date independently), and the final destruction of its corrupted, renunciate remnant in 1388. This is the single most detailed account of one specific August Court's internal history and endpoint anywhere in this library.
- Net effect: read together, these five books give a full 800-year institutional history for a single political structure that any one of them alone only shows a cross-section of.

### 2. Yama Kings, akuma, and the Thousand Hells
- **kote-core** (Ch.1, Ch.6) introduces the Yama Kings and akuma as concepts, names a handful of Yama Kings in passing (Mikaboshi, Emma-o, Tou Mu, Rangda), and sketches three Yomi Hell-realms (Wicked City, Kakuri, Hell of Being Skinned Alive) in a few paragraphs each.
- **kote-1000-hells** expands every one of those same named Yama Kings into full profiles with origin myths, domains, and signature servants (Emma-o's shikome, Mikaboshi's tech-adaptation, Tou Mu's living Iron Panoply, Rangda's disease magic) — plus adds Ravana and the foundational myth of Yen-Lo, "First of the Fallen," explaining *why* Yomi has multiple competing Yama Kings at all (Yen-Lo's originally singular, just realm of punishment was carved up after his fellow Yama Kings assassinated him).
- **kote-blood-silk** (Ch.1-2) adds its own named fallen ancestor, **Bao Zhong**, one of the four last Wan Xian who chose Yomi Wan over Heaven and eventually declared himself a Yama King ruling the "Hell of Never-Dying Sorrow" — Blood & Silk (Ch.2) lists him as one of the most powerful Yama Kings of its era alongside Mikaboshi, Ravana, Emma-o, Rangda, and Tou Mu, but he does not appear in 1000 Hells or the core book (verified by cross-book search) — an example of a named Yama King unique to a single volume rather than one recurring across the whole line. Blood & Silk *does* separately confirm that Qin Shihuangdi's afterlife as Yu Huang, ruler of the Yellow Springs, is a distinct figure from the "true" Yama Kings (matching 1000 Hells' Introduction distinction between Yomi and Yu Huang's artificial Ti Yu).
- **kote-companion** (Ch.1, Golden Courts section) shows Rangda specifically as an active antagonist *outside* Yomi, working through the Golden Courts' akuma infiltration — giving a present-day political consequence to a threat 1000 Hells treats mostly as cosmological backdrop.
- **kote-shadow-war** (Ch.2) treats "dealing with the Yama Kings" as one of the most forbidden midnight-war tactics (bartering for aid in Hiruko, the "Hell of Unspeakable Delights") — the taboo that 1000 Hells' Appendix explains mechanically (infernalism as a capital offense, vs. legal demonology).
- Akuma treatment deepens book by book: **core** introduces the term; **companion** (Ch.2) adds the sociological nuance that "akuma" is often just a political slur; **1000 hells** (Appendix) gives the fullest mechanical/narrative treatment — pact rituals (the Black Peony Chant), named Treatments, and named akuma NPCs (Tashimoto Hanko, Miriam Weatherbee — the latter explicitly tied back to the Companion's Golden Courts/Penangallan material); **blood & silk** (Ch.1) gives a three-part akuma taxonomy (Reluctant/Aggressive/Hidden) predating the modern categorization.
- **wind-from-the-east** (Ch.2) adds its own named Yama King, **Dokhor-Khan**, "Lord of the Hell of Spiked Chains," identified as the demon primarily responsible for corrupting the northern steppe's dragon nests and the Black Tortoise Court's akuma specifically (with corruption rates rising from roughly 1-in-12 in 1197 to 1-in-2 by the Quincunx era) — a Yama King unique to this volume, in the same pattern as Blood & Silk's Bao Zhong (a named figure who doesn't recur elsewhere in the library, rather than a contradiction).

### 3. Dharmas: orthodox, heretical, and historical
- **kote-core** (Ch.2) establishes the five orthodox Dharmas (Devil-Tiger/P'o, Resplendent Crane/Hun, Song of the Shadow/Yin, Thousand Whispers/Balance, Thrashing Dragon/Yang), all tracing to the arhat Xue, with a full Dharma-rating game system (0–10, satori/dên vs. diao).
- **kote-companion** (Ch.2) adds four *heretical* Dharmas rejected by Quincunx orthodoxy — Flame of the Rising Phoenix, Tempest of Inward Focus, The Face of Gods, Spirit of the Living Earth — each structurally mirroring the five orthodox paths (same entry format: Virtue, training, weakness, tenets).
- **kote-blood-silk** (Ch.2) shows the Dharma system as it existed roughly 800 years earlier, including a Fourth Age-specific ratings chart and a note that some heretical Dharmas (Spirit of the Living Earth specifically) don't yet exist as practiced traditions in this era — meaning the Companion's heresies formed at different points across Kuei-jin history, not all at once.
- **kote-1000-hells** doesn't add new Dharmas but shows how radically harder Dharmic balance (Hun vs. P'o) becomes inside Yomi itself (Ch.5) — the environment structurally favors the P'o, complicating the very system Ch.2 of the core book establishes.

### 4. Generational conflict: Running Monkeys and the Bamboo Princes
- **kote-core** (Ch.1) mentions "Running Monkeys" (young Kuei-jin under ~40) mostly as a slang term and doesn't develop the generational tension further.
- **kote-companion** (Ch.1, Golden Courts/Singapore section) shows early hints of generational political friction (younger Kuei-jin banished to Singapore for challenging Penangallan authority).
- **kote-shadow-war** (Ch.3) is the fullest treatment: an entire chapter on the **Bamboo Princes** (Takeouji/Zhu Wangzi), an organized youth-modernization movement founded around the figure Burakumin, with its own slang, cell structure (Ichimi), and guerrilla tactics against elder mandarins.
- **kote-blood-silk** (Ch.1) shows this tension in embryonic form — "the seeds of conflict" section explicitly frames the coming Fifth Age's young-vs-old strife as something the Fourth Age narrator can already see building.
- Net effect: this is one of the clearest examples in the library of a single thread — generational conflict — that starts as a two-word aside in the core book and ends up as this entire supplement's organizing subject.

### 5. Discipline lineage: Godbody → Shintai
- **kote-core** (Ch.4) presents the five Shintai Disciplines (Blood/Bone/Jade/Flesh/Ghost-Flame) as simply how Kuei-jin power works, with no historical framing.
- **kote-blood-silk** (Ch.4) reveals these as the *evolved* Fifth Age form of five Fourth Age **Godbody Disciplines** (Godbody of Earth/Fire/Metal/Water/Wood) — explicitly noting Ghost-Flame Shintai doesn't exist yet in 1197 CE, while Beast/Smoke/Storm Shintai (introduced as *new* material in the Companion) already do.
- **kote-companion** (Ch.3) expands the Fifth Age Shintai roster further (Beast, Smoke, Storm Chintai) and adds two new non-elemental Soul Arts (Mibasham, Tzu Wei) — none of which Blood & Silk's Fourth Age framing accounts for, implying these arose sometime in the intervening ~800 years.
- **kote-1000-hells** (Ch.5) shows how Yomi itself distorts all of these Disciplines regardless of era — a system-level constant layered on top of the historical variation the other three books document.

### 6. East vs. West: Kuei-jin and Kindred contact
- **kote-core** (Ch.7, Ch.8) frames Kin-jin (Kindred) contact as a storytelling theme and antagonist category, largely at arm's length.
- **kote-companion** (Ch.6, "Gweilo") gives the fullest systematic survey — region-by-region Kin-jin activity in Asia, and Kuei-jin operating abroad (Middle East, Europe, North America), plus the kanbujian (Kuei-jin born outside the Middle Kingdom, cut off from Dharma).
- **kote-shadow-war** (Ch.5, "Crimson War") escalates this from background tension to an active, chapter-length war narrative: a sudden Kuei-jin offensive against the Kindred Anarch Free States of California, with named factions (Ancestor Ch'ang/Blood Court vs. della Passaglia) and a region-by-region campaign map (LA, San Diego, Hawaii, Europe, etc.) — the most concrete depiction of Kuei-jin activity in the West anywhere in the library.
- **kote-blood-silk** (Ch.6) shows the *earliest* layer of this contact, historically — 12th-13th century missionary-Kindred (referencing the real Franciscan friar Giovanni de Plano Carpini) making first contact, centuries before the modern hostility documented in the Companion and Shadow War fully calcified.
- **wind-from-the-east** independently references the same Giovanni de Plano Carpini mission (Ch.1, a sidebar built around the real 1245-46 embassy to Karakorum) — the only case in this library of two books citing the identical real-world historical event, one (Blood & Silk) from the Kuei-jin side in passing and one (Wind from the East) from the Cainite side in detail. Wind from the East's whole Ch.1 (pp.16-45) is in effect a book-length account of exactly this kind of East-West vampire contact, just narrated from the Cainite side of the encounter that the other four books narrate from the Kuei-jin side.
- Read chronologically (Blood & Silk/Wind from the East → core/Companion → Shadow War), these five books trace East-West vampire contact from tentative medieval first contact through Cold War-era wariness to open 1990s warfare.

### 7. A Cainite mirror: the Anda, Wu Zao, and the view from outside the Kuei-jin line
- Uniquely among this library's books, **wind-from-the-east** is not narrated from the Kuei-jin/Wan Kuei perspective at all — it introduces the **Anda** (a lost offshoot of Clan Gangrel, Embraced from among the Mongol tribes and cursed with the same restless mobility their steppe-nomad herds practice) and the **Wu Zao** (a lost offshoot of Clan Salubri, descended from Zao-lat — the Wu Zao's own name for Saulot) as *Cainite* answers to the "hidden vampire species of Asia" concept the other five books explore from the Kuei-jin side.
- This gives the library a genuine second axis: where **kote-core** Ch.8 and **kote-companion** Ch.6 briefly mention Kin-jin/Western vampires as rivals or curiosities glimpsed from the Kuei-jin side, **wind-from-the-east** shows what it looks like from the other direction — including the Anda's own origin myth (Dobrul the Brave, cursed by a "Xia shaman" after defending his family from "Xia Demons," Ch.1) as a mirror-image, Cainite-flavored retelling of the same Wan-Kuei-as-demonic-threat framing found throughout the *Kindred of the East* line.
- The **Black Tortoise Court** is the direct hinge between the two perspectives: it is the one Wan Kuei institution given full treatment in *both* a *Kindred of the East*-line book (implicitly, via the core book's Ch.6 and Ch.8 mentions) and an outside *Vampire: The Dark Ages* book (**wind-from-the-east** Ch.2) — and the two accounts are consistent rather than contradictory, describing the same Court from opposite sides of its central conflict (the extermination of the Anda).

## Points of agreement
- All five books share one consistent cosmology: the Wheel of Ages/Great Cycle, the Yin/Yang Worlds and Yomi as a third, corrupted realm, the Hun/P'o dual-soul structure, and the Yama Kings as demonic antagonists — no book contradicts another on these fundamentals.
- The five orthodox Dharmas and their tenets are identical across every book that discusses them (core, Companion, Blood & Silk).
- Akuma are consistently defined as Kuei-jin in service to the Yama Kings across all five books, with increasing sociological/mechanical nuance added by each successive volume (see Theme 2 above) rather than any contradiction.
- Named Yama Kings (Mikaboshi, Emma-o, Tou Mu, Rangda) have stable portfolios and personalities across the core book, Companion, 1000 Hells, and Blood & Silk.
- **wind-from-the-east**'s account of the Black Tortoise Court, the Five August Courts, the Dharma system, and Yama Kings/akuma is fully consistent with the other five books wherever it touches the same ground — notably, its own "A Primer on the Middle Kingdom" sidebar (Introduction) is an explicit, self-aware condensation of *Kindred of the East* core-book material for readers new to the line, not an independent (and potentially conflicting) reinvention of it.

## Points of disagreement or tension
- **Discipline availability across eras**: Blood & Silk explicitly states Ghost-Flame Shintai doesn't exist in the Fourth Age (1197 CE), while the core rulebook (set in the modern Fifth Age) presents it as standard. This isn't a contradiction so much as an intentional historical gap the books leave for Storytellers to fill — none of the five books state exactly when Ghost-Flame Shintai, or the Companion's Beast/Smoke/Storm Shintai and Mibasham/Tzu Wei Soul Arts, were actually developed.
- **Scale and number of the Yama Kings**: 1000 Hells' Ch.3 explicitly notes in-world disagreement over whether there are a handful of "prominent" Yama Kings or literally thousands (one per hell) — this uncertainty is presented as unresolved lore rather than an authorial inconsistency, and no other book in the library attempts to settle it.
- **The Quincunx's authority is more absolute in the Companion's founding-treaty text than it appears in practice elsewhere**: the Treaty of the Quincunx (Companion Ch.1) reads as a sweeping, binding law over "all Wan Kuei throughout the Middle Kingdom," but the core book (Ch.1, Ch.6) and Shadow War (Ch.3, the Bamboo Princes) both depict extensive defiance, independent courts, and outright rebellion against Quincunx authority — the books are consistent in showing the Treaty's *aspiration* to absolute authority against the *reality* of a much messier, contested political landscape.
- **Golden Courts characterization**: the Companion (Ch.1) frames Quincunx hostility toward the Golden Courts as largely xenophobic prejudice ("barbarians," "heathens") while also documenting real, severe akuma infiltration there (tied to Rangda) — the book holds both a sympathetic and a genuinely alarming view of the same region in tension, rather than resolving which framing is "correct."

## Chronology / influence
- **In-world chronology**: *Blood & Silk* is set in 1197 CE (end of the Fourth Age); the core rulebook, Companion, Shadow War, and 1000 Hells are all set in the contemporary(-to-1998) Fifth Age, roughly 800 years later. Blood & Silk is thus a prequel across the whole line, not a parallel or sequel volume. *Wind from the East* spans 1166-1388 — its earliest material overlaps almost exactly with Blood & Silk's 1197 CE snapshot (its own "Tribes of the Khans" section is explicitly dated "In 1197..."), then continues forward through the Black Tortoise Court's 1304 Quincunx accession and 1388 dissolution, functionally bridging Blood & Silk's Fourth Age snapshot and the modern Fifth Age setting of the other four books.
- **Real-world publication order**: *Kindred of the East* (1998) → *Kindred of the East Companion* → *Shadow War* (1999) → *The Thousand Hells* (1999) → *World of Darkness: Blood & Silk* (2000) → *Wind from the East* (2000, *Vampire: The Dark Ages* line). This matches the pattern visible in the text itself: later books consistently assume and build on earlier ones (the Companion explicitly updates core-book material for a new *Vampire* rules edition; Shadow War and 1000 Hells both assume the reader knows the core book's Dharmas and Yama King names; Blood & Silk is written last among the *Kindred of the East* line and functions as a capstone explaining the *origins* of almost everything the other four books treat as settled fact; Wind from the East, published the same year as Blood & Silk but for a different game line, explicitly cites both *Kindred of the East* and *Blood & Silk* as source material in its own Introduction).
- Cross-references are frequent and consistent: 1000 Hells' Introduction explicitly distinguishes its subject from *Dark Kingdom of Jade* (an external, non-library book about the wraith afterlife); Blood & Silk's Ch.1 confirms and extends that same distinction from the "prequel" side; Wind from the East's Introduction explicitly names both *Kindred of the East* and *Blood & Silk* as source material a Storyteller may want alongside it.

## Cross-book query examples
Run these from the `library/` directory (paths are relative to it). The query string is the last positional argument, before any `--limit` flag:
```
python3 scripts/query_library.py kote-core/book_chunks.db kote-companion/book_chunks.db kote-shadow-war/book_chunks.db kote-1000-hells/book_chunks.db kote-blood-silk/book_chunks.db wind-from-the-east/book_chunks.db "Quincunx" --limit 3
```
```
python3 scripts/query_library.py kote-core/book_chunks.db kote-1000-hells/book_chunks.db kote-blood-silk/book_chunks.db "Rangda"
```
```
python3 scripts/query_library.py kote-companion/book_chunks.db kote-shadow-war/book_chunks.db "Bamboo Princes"
```
```
python3 scripts/query_library.py wind-from-the-east/book_chunks.db kote-blood-silk/book_chunks.db "Giovanni de Plano Carpini"
```
The slug names shown (e.g. `kote-core`) are what label each hit in the grouped results. A live test of the first query above confirmed the cross-referencing described in this file — e.g. it surfaced Blood & Silk's own note that "the great court purges and the formation of the Quincunx lie in the future of Blood & Silk, appearing only as disturbing dreams for the prophetic," directly confirming the Chronology section above.

`kote-relentless-age` is deliberately never mixed into the queries above — its "Quincunx" and "Mikaboshi" are different entities under the same names (see "Important: kote-relentless-age is a different, non-canonical edition"). Query it on its own instead:
```
python3 scripts/query_library.py kote-relentless-age/book_chunks.db "Quincunx"
```
